## About the Acceleration Calculator

**Acceleration** is the rate at which an object's velocity changes over time. It is a fundamental concept in classical mechanics and is directly related to force through Newton's Second Law. Acceleration can mean speeding up, slowing down (deceleration), or changing direction.

---

## The Formulas

**From velocity and time:**
$$ a = \frac{\Delta v}{\Delta t} = \frac{v_f - v_i}{t} $$

**From Newton's Second Law:**
$$ a = \frac{F}{m} $$

**From kinematic equations:**
$$ v_f^2 = v_i^2 + 2a \cdot d $$

Where:
- **$a$** = Acceleration (m/s²)
- **$v_f$** = Final velocity (m/s)
- **$v_i$** = Initial velocity (m/s)
- **$t$** = Time (s)
- **$d$** = Distance (m)

**Example:** A car accelerates from 0 to 30 m/s in 10 seconds:
$$ a = \frac{30 - 0}{10} = 3 \text{ m/s}^2 $$

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is deceleration?**
Deceleration is simply negative acceleration — the object is slowing down. In physics, we do not typically use "deceleration" as a separate term; an acceleration value of −5 m/s² means the object is slowing at 5 m/s per second.

**Can an object accelerate while moving at constant speed?**
Yes! If an object moves in a circle at constant speed, it is continuously accelerating because its *direction* is changing. This is called centripetal acceleration: $a_c = \frac{v^2}{r}$.
