## About the APY Calculator (Annual Percentage Yield)

**APY** (Annual Percentage Yield) is the true annual rate of return on a savings account or investment, accounting for the effect of compound interest throughout the year. Unlike the nominal interest rate, APY gives you an apples-to-apples comparison between accounts that compound at different frequencies.

---

## APY Formula

$$ APY = \left(1 + \frac{r}{n}\right)^n - 1 $$

Where:
- **$r$** = Nominal annual interest rate (as a decimal)
- **$n$** = Number of compounding periods per year

**Example:** A savings account offers a 5% nominal rate compounded monthly:
$$ APY = \left(1 + \frac{0.05}{12}\right)^{12} - 1 = (1.004167)^{12} - 1 \approx 0.05116 = 5.116\% $$

Even though the advertised rate is 5%, you actually earn **5.116% APY** due to monthly compounding.

## Compounding Frequency Comparison (at 5% nominal rate)

| Compounding | APY |
|:---|:---|
| Annually | 5.000% |
| Quarterly | 5.094% |
| Monthly | 5.116% |
| Daily | 5.127% |
| Continuously | 5.127% |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the difference between APR and APY?**
APR (Annual Percentage Rate) is used for borrowing — it is the nominal rate that does NOT account for compounding. APY is used for savings and investments and DOES account for compounding. When borrowing, a lower APR is better. When saving, a higher APY is better.

**Is APY the same as effective annual rate (EAR)?**
Yes. APY and EAR are the same concept with different names. Banks use "APY" for deposits (US regulation requires it for transparency). Finance textbooks use "EAR" for the same calculation.
