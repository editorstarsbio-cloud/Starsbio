## About the Annual Percentage Yield (APY) Calculator

When you put money in a savings account, banks quote you two numbers: the Interest Rate and the APY (Annual Percentage Yield). The **APY Calculator** explains why those numbers are different and shows you exactly how much your money will grow over a year.

---

## Interest Rate vs. APY

- **Interest Rate (or APR):** The flat, base rate the bank pays you. It ignores compounding.
- **APY:** The *real* rate of return you earn over a year, because it includes the effect of compound interest (earning interest on your interest).

Because banks want their savings accounts to look as attractive as possible, they always advertise the APY in large font, because it is mathematically higher than the base interest rate.

---

## The Formula

$$ \text{APY} = \left(1 + \frac{r}{n}\right)^n - 1 $$

Where:
- **$r$** = The stated nominal interest rate (as a decimal)
- **$n$** = The number of compounding periods per year

**Example:**
A bank offers a 5.00% interest rate, compounded daily ($n = 365$).
$$ \text{APY} = \left(1 + \frac{0.05}{365}\right)^{365} - 1 $$
$$ \text{APY} = (1.0001369)^{365} - 1 = 1.05126 - 1 = \mathbf{5.126\%} $$

A $10,000 deposit will earn exactly $512.60 in one year.

---

## The Effect of Compounding Frequency

The more frequently the bank calculates and deposits interest into your account, the higher your APY will be.
Assuming a flat 5.00% base interest rate:
- **Compounded Annually:** APY = 5.000%
- **Compounded Quarterly:** APY = 5.094%
- **Compounded Monthly:** APY = 5.116%
- **Compounded Daily:** APY = 5.126%

Most modern High-Yield Savings Accounts (HYSAs) compound interest daily and pay it out monthly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why does my loan use APR while my savings account uses APY?**
Marketing and psychology. APY is higher than APR. Therefore, banks use APY for savings accounts to make it look like they are paying you a lot, and they use APR for loans (like car loans or mortgages) to make it look like they are charging you very little. Both involve compounding, but they selectively advertise the number that looks best for them.

**Does APY account for inflation?**
No. APY is a nominal rate. If your savings account has an APY of 4%, but inflation is 3%, your "Real APY" (actual increase in purchasing power) is only 1%. If inflation is 5%, you are actually losing purchasing power despite earning interest.
