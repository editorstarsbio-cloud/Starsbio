## How to Split a Bill Fairly

There are generally three ways to handle a group check:

### 1. The Even Split
The easiest method. You take the grand total (including tax and tip) and divide it equally by the number of people.
$$ \text{Per Person} = \frac{\text{Total Bill} + \text{Total Tip}}{\text{Number of People}} $$
*Best for:* Groups where everyone ordered roughly the same amount of food and drinks.

### 2. The Itemized Split (Proportional)
The fairest method, but requires more math. Everyone pays for exactly what they ordered, plus their proportional share of the tax and tip.
$$ \text{Person's Share of Tax/Tip} = \left(\frac{\text{Person's Subtotal}}{\text{Total Subtotal}}\right) \times (\text{Total Tax} + \text{Total Tip}) $$
$$ \text{Person's Total} = \text{Person's Subtotal} + \text{Person's Share of Tax/Tip} $$
*Best for:* Groups where one person ordered a salad and water, and another ordered a steak and three cocktails.

### 3. Splitting with Couples
If a group consists of two couples and one single person, dividing by 5 might not make sense if a couple wants to pay together. You can treat the couple as "2 shares" and the single person as "1 share."

## Tipping When Splitting

When splitting a bill, it is crucial to calculate the tip based on the **entire total** before breaking it apart. If a group of 4 splits a $100 bill ($25 each), and everyone throws in $2 extra for the tip, the total tip is only $8 (an abysmal 8%). 

Always calculate the 20% tip on the $100 first ($20), making the grand total $120. Then divide by 4 ($30 each).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is it rude to ask for separate checks?**
It depends on the restaurant and the party size. In casual dining, it is perfectly fine, but you should ideally ask the server *before* you order so they can organize their ticketing system. In fine dining, or for parties larger than 6, many restaurants refuse to split checks or limit it to a maximum of 2 or 3 credit cards.

**How do we handle the person who didn't drink alcohol?**
Alcohol severely skews restaurant bills. The fairest approach is to separate the alcohol subtotal from the food subtotal. The drinkers split the alcohol cost (plus proportional tax/tip), and everyone splits the food cost.
