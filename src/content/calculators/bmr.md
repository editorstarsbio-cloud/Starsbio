## About the BMR Calculator (Basal Metabolic Rate)

Your **Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR)** is the number of calories your body burns each day while completely at rest. Think of it as your body's "idle fuel consumption." Even while sleeping, your body is working hard — pumping blood, breathing, regulating temperature, and repairing cells. BMR accounts for 60–75% of all the calories you burn every day.

Understanding your BMR is the essential first step for anyone trying to lose weight, gain muscle, or maintain their current body composition.

---

## How to Calculate BMR

The two most widely used formulas for estimating BMR are the **Mifflin-St Jeor equation** (most accurate for the general population) and the older **Harris-Benedict equation**.

### Mifflin-St Jeor Equation

**For Men:**
$$ \text{BMR} = (10 \times \text{weight in kg}) + (6.25 \times \text{height in cm}) - (5 \times \text{age}) + 5 $$

**For Women:**
$$ \text{BMR} = (10 \times \text{weight in kg}) + (6.25 \times \text{height in cm}) - (5 \times \text{age}) - 161 $$

**Example (Male, 28 years old, 75 kg, 178 cm):**
$$ \text{BMR} = (10 \times 75) + (6.25 \times 178) - (5 \times 28) + 5 $$
$$ \text{BMR} = 750 + 1112.5 - 140 + 5 = 1727.5 \text{ kcal/day} $$

This means the body burns approximately **1,728 calories per day at complete rest**.

## BMR vs. TDEE

**BMR** is the calories burned at absolute rest. **TDEE** (Total Daily Energy Expenditure) is your BMR multiplied by an activity factor, giving you the total calories burned across a full day of living.

| Activity Level | Multiplier | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Sedentary | × 1.2 | Desk job, no exercise |
| Lightly Active | × 1.375 | Light exercise 1–3 days/week |
| Moderately Active | × 1.55 | Exercise 3–5 days/week |
| Very Active | × 1.725 | Hard exercise 6–7 days/week |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does BMR change as I age?**
Yes. BMR tends to decrease by roughly 1–2% per decade after age 20. This occurs because adults gradually lose muscle mass with age (a process called sarcopenia), and muscle tissue burns far more calories at rest than fat tissue does.

**Is this calculator a medical tool?**
No. BMR calculations are estimates based on statistical population averages. Individual metabolism varies significantly based on genetics, hormonal conditions (like thyroid function), and body composition. Always consult a registered dietitian or physician before making major dietary changes.
