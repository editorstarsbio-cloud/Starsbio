## About the Business Valuation Calculator

**Business Valuation** is the process of determining the economic value of a whole business or company unit. Whether you are looking to sell your company, bring in investors, buy out a partner, or establish equity for employees, our **Business Valuation Calculator** provides baseline estimates using standard industry multiples.

## Common Valuation Methods

There is no single "correct" way to value a business. Different buyers use different methods. The three most common are:

### 1. Revenue Multiple Method
This method is common for fast-growing startups, SaaS companies, and professional practices (like accounting or medical firms) that may not yet have optimized profitability.
$$ \text{Value} = \text{Annual Revenue} \times \text{Industry Multiplier} $$
*Example:* A SaaS company doing $2M in ARR with an industry multiple of 5x is valued at $10M.

### 2. SDE (Seller's Discretionary Earnings) Multiple
This is the standard for small businesses (restaurants, local retail). SDE is the profit of the business *plus* the owner's salary and any personal expenses run through the business.
$$ \text{Value} = \text{SDE} \times \text{Multiplier (usually 1.5x to 3x)} $$
*Example:* A bakery generates $150k in profit, and the owner pays themselves $50k. SDE = $200k. At a 2x multiple, the business is worth $400k.

### 3. EBITDA Multiple
Used for mid-market and larger companies. EBITDA stands for Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation, and Amortization. It measures pure operational profitability.
$$ \text{Value} = \text{EBITDA} \times \text{Multiplier (usually 4x to 8x)} $$

## What Determines the Multiplier?

Why does one business sell for 2x earnings while another sells for 6x? The multiplier is driven by risk and growth potential:

- **High Multipliers (e.g., 5x - 10x):** High year-over-year growth, strong recurring revenue (subscriptions), high barriers to entry, patented IP, and a business that runs completely independently of the owner.
- **Low Multipliers (e.g., 1x - 2x):** Declining industry, heavy reliance on a single major client, high customer churn, and a business that collapses if the owner takes a vacation.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does the valuation include cash in the bank and inventory?**
Small business sales are usually structured as "asset sales." In standard SDE multiples, inventory is typically *included* in the price. Cash and accounts receivable are usually kept by the seller, and the seller pays off all debts before transferring the business. 

**What is the Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) method?**
DCF is a highly complex valuation method used by investment banks for large corporations. It projects the business's cash flows 5 to 10 years into the future, calculates a terminal value, and discounts everything back to today's dollars using a required rate of return. It is theoretically the most accurate method but relies heavily on highly speculative future projections.
