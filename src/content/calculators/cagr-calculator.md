## About the CAGR Calculator

**Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR)** is the smoothed annual growth rate of an investment or business metric over a period longer than one year. It answers the question: "If this investment had grown at a perfectly steady rate each year, what would that rate be?"

---

## The Formula

$$ \text{CAGR} = \left(\frac{\text{End Value}}{\text{Start Value}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}} - 1 $$

Where $n$ is the number of years.

**Example:** A business grew revenue from $500,000 to $1,200,000 over 6 years:
$$ \text{CAGR} = \left(\frac{1,200,000}{500,000}\right)^{\frac{1}{6}} - 1 $$
$$ \text{CAGR} = (2.4)^{0.1667} - 1 \approx 1.157 - 1 = 15.7\% $$

The business grew at a compound annual growth rate of **15.7% per year**.

---

## CAGR vs. Average Annual Growth Rate

CAGR is NOT the same as a simple average annual growth rate. Consider an investment:
- Year 1: +100% (doubles from $100 to $200)
- Year 2: −50% (halves back to $100)

Simple average: $(100\% + (-50\%)) / 2 = 25\%$ — misleadingly positive!
CAGR: $(\frac{100}{100})^{1/2} - 1 = 0\%$ — accurately shows zero actual gain.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What CAGR does the stock market historically return?**
The S&P 500 has historically delivered a CAGR of approximately 10% (nominal) or 7% (inflation-adjusted) over long periods. Individual stocks vary wildly.

**Can CAGR be negative?**
Yes. If an investment loses value, CAGR is negative. A $10,000 investment that fell to $6,000 over 5 years has a CAGR of $(\frac{6000}{10000})^{0.2} - 1 \approx -9.6\%$ per year.
