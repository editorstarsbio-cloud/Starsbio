## About the Calorie Calculator (TDEE)

Our **Calorie Calculator** is a highly accurate tool designed to estimate the exact number of calories your body burns in a day, also known as your Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE). Whether your goal is to lose weight, build muscle, or maintain your current physique, understanding your daily caloric needs is the absolute most important step.

Weight management is governed by the scientific law of thermodynamics. If you consume more calories than you burn, you gain weight (a caloric surplus). If you consume fewer calories than you burn, you lose weight (a caloric deficit). This calculator removes the guesswork from your diet planning.

## How Does the Calorie Calculator Work?

To determine your daily calorie needs, the calculator first estimates your Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR). Your BMR represents the number of calories your body requires just to stay alive at rest (breathing, circulating blood, cellular repair). 

Once your BMR is calculated, it is multiplied by an **Activity Multiplier** to account for the physical movement you do throughout the day (walking, exercising, working).

### The Mifflin-St Jeor Equation

While there are several formulas to calculate BMR, the Mifflin-St Jeor equation is considered by health professionals and the American Dietetic Association to be the most accurate for the general population.

**For Men:**
$$ \text{BMR} = (10 \times \text{weight in kg}) + (6.25 \times \text{height in cm}) - (5 \times \text{age in years}) + 5 $$

**For Women:**
$$ \text{BMR} = (10 \times \text{weight in kg}) + (6.25 \times \text{height in cm}) - (5 \times \text{age in years}) - 161 $$

**Example Calculation:**
A 30-year-old man who weighs 80 kg and is 180 cm tall.
$$ \text{BMR} = (10 \times 80) + (6.25 \times 180) - (5 \times 30) + 5 $$
$$ \text{BMR} = 800 + 1125 - 150 + 5 = 1780 \text{ calories/day} $$

### Applying the Activity Multiplier

Once the BMR (1780) is found, we multiply it by the activity level:
- Sedentary (little to no exercise): BMR × 1.2
- Lightly active (light exercise 1-3 days/week): BMR × 1.375
- Moderately active (moderate exercise 3-5 days/week): BMR × 1.55
- Very active (hard exercise 6-7 days/week): BMR × 1.725
- Extra active (very hard exercise/physical job): BMR × 1.9

If our example man exercises moderately, his TDEE is:
$$ 1780 \times 1.55 = 2759 \text{ calories/day} $$

## Setting Your Caloric Goals

Once you know your TDEE, you can adjust your diet based on your goals:

### For Weight Loss
To lose weight safely, health experts recommend creating a deficit of 500 calories per day. This equates to about 1 pound (0.45 kg) of fat loss per week.
$$ \text{Target Calories} = \text{TDEE} - 500 $$

### For Muscle Gain (Bulking)
To build muscle efficiently without gaining excessive fat, aim for a slight caloric surplus of 250 to 500 calories per day, combined with progressive resistance training.
$$ \text{Target Calories} = \text{TDEE} + 250 $$

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Are all calories equal?**
For strict weight loss, a calorie is a calorie. However, for body composition and overall health, where you get your calories matters immensely. 500 calories of protein will keep you full, preserve muscle, and burn slightly more energy to digest compared to 500 calories of refined sugar.

**Why is my weight loss stalling?**
As you lose weight, your body requires less energy to sustain itself. This means your BMR and TDEE will drop over time. If you hit a plateau, you must recalculate your calorie needs using your new, lower body weight.

**Should I eat back the calories I burn from exercise?**
Generally, no. Fitness trackers and cardio machines notoriously overestimate calories burned by up to 30%. If you selected an active lifestyle in the calculator, those exercise calories are already factored into your daily total. Eating them back will often erase your caloric deficit.
