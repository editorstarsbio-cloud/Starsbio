## About the Calorie Calculator

Our **Calorie Calculator** estimates your Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) — the total number of calories your body burns every day. This is the definitive starting number for any fat loss, muscle-building, or weight maintenance plan.

> **Disclaimer:** This tool produces statistical estimates, not medical prescriptions. Individual metabolism varies. Consult a registered dietitian or physician before making significant dietary changes.

---

## How TDEE is Calculated

TDEE is your Basal Metabolic Rate (BMR) multiplied by an activity factor.

**Step 1 — Mifflin-St Jeor BMR (most accurate formula):**

**Men:** $\text{BMR} = (10 \times \text{kg}) + (6.25 \times \text{cm}) - (5 \times \text{age}) + 5$

**Women:** $\text{BMR} = (10 \times \text{kg}) + (6.25 \times \text{cm}) - (5 \times \text{age}) - 161$

**Step 2 — Multiply by Activity Factor:**

| Activity Level | Multiplier |
|:---|:---|
| Sedentary (desk job) | × 1.2 |
| Lightly Active (1–3 days/week exercise) | × 1.375 |
| Moderately Active (3–5 days/week) | × 1.55 |
| Very Active (6–7 days/week hard exercise) | × 1.725 |

**Example:** A 35-year-old woman, 65 kg, 165 cm, moderately active:
$$ \text{BMR} = (10 \times 65) + (6.25 \times 165) - (5 \times 35) - 161 = 1383.25 $$
$$ \text{TDEE} = 1383.25 \times 1.55 = \approx 2144 \text{ kcal/day} $$

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How accurate are calorie counts on food labels?**
The FDA allows a ±20% margin of error on nutrition label calorie counts. Combined with variations in individual metabolism and cooking methods, calorie tracking is inherently approximate. Treat your TDEE as a starting estimate and adjust based on real results over 2–4 weeks.

**Do I need to eat back exercise calories?**
If you selected your activity level accurately (including exercise), those calories are already counted in your TDEE. You should not eat them back additionally.
