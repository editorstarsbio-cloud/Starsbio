## About the Cap Rate Calculator

The **Capitalization Rate (Cap Rate)** is the most fundamental metric in real estate investment analysis. It tells you the annual rate of return a property generates from its income alone, independent of financing. Investors use it to compare different properties on equal footing.

---

## The Formula

$$ \text{Cap Rate} = \frac{\text{Net Operating Income (NOI)}}{\text{Property Value}} \times 100 $$

Where:
- **Net Operating Income (NOI)** = Gross Rental Income − All Operating Expenses (property taxes, insurance, maintenance, property management, vacancy allowance). Does NOT include mortgage payments.

**Example:**
- Gross annual rent: $24,000
- Operating expenses: $8,000
- NOI: $24,000 − $8,000 = **$16,000**
- Property purchase price: $200,000

$$ \text{Cap Rate} = \frac{16,000}{200,000} \times 100 = 8\% $$

---

## What is a Good Cap Rate?

Cap rate benchmarks vary by property type and location:

| Property Type | Typical Cap Rate |
|:---|:---|
| Class A Urban Apartment | 3–5% |
| Suburban Residential | 5–7% |
| Commercial / Retail | 5–8% |
| Industrial / Warehouse | 6–9% |
| Rural / Higher-Risk | 8–12%+ |

A lower cap rate means a safer asset in a high-demand area. A higher cap rate signals higher potential returns but also higher risk.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does cap rate include mortgage payments?**
No. Cap rate is calculated on NOI, which specifically excludes debt service (mortgage principal and interest). This makes it a pure measure of the property's income-generating performance, independent of how you finance it.

**How is cap rate different from cash-on-cash return?**
Cap rate ignores financing. Cash-on-cash return measures the actual cash income relative to the cash you invested (your down payment + closing costs), making it a post-financing metric. Use cap rate to compare properties; use cash-on-cash return to evaluate your actual investment performance.
