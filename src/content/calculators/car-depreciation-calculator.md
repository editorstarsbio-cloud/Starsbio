## About the Car Depreciation Calculator

**Car depreciation** is the largest hidden cost of vehicle ownership. It represents the loss of value a vehicle experiences over time due to age, wear and tear, and market conditions. Our **Car Depreciation Calculator** helps you project the future resale value of your vehicle.

---

## How Fast Do Cars Depreciate?

Depreciation is not linear. Vehicles lose value fastest in their first few years. While every model is different, a standard depreciation curve looks like this:

| Timeframe | Cumulative Value Loss |
|:---|:---|
| Minute driven off lot | 9% – 11% |
| Year 1 | 20% – 25% |
| Year 2 | 30% – 35% |
| Year 3 | 40% – 45% |
| Year 5 | 50% – 60% |

## The Formula

To calculate estimated future value using a constant depreciation rate:

$$ V = P \times (1 - r)^t $$

Where:
- **$V$** = Future Value
- **$P$** = Purchase Price (or current value)
- **$r$** = Annual depreciation rate (as a decimal)
- **$t$** = Number of years

**Example:** A $35,000 car depreciating at 15% per year over 3 years:
$$ V = 35000 \times (1 - 0.15)^3 $$
$$ V = 35000 \times (0.85)^3 = 35000 \times 0.614 = \$21,494 $$
Total depreciation = $35,000 - $21,494 = **$13,506**.

## Factors That Accelerate Depreciation

- **High Mileage:** Driving more than the standard 12,000 - 15,000 miles per year.
- **Condition:** Dents, interior wear, and mechanical issues.
- **Brand Reputation:** Luxury vehicles and brands with poor reliability records depreciate faster.
- **Multiple Owners:** A car with 3 owners will be worth less than a single-owner car.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Which cars hold their value best?**
Historically, reliable brands like Toyota, Honda, and Subaru experience the slowest depreciation. Certain segments, like midsize pickup trucks (e.g., Toyota Tacoma) and rugged SUVs (e.g., Jeep Wrangler), hold their value exceptionally well.

**Is it better to buy a new or used car to avoid depreciation?**
Buying a 2- to 3-year-old used car is the classic financial advice for avoiding the steepest part of the depreciation curve. You let the first owner absorb the initial 30-40% value loss, while still getting a modern vehicle.
