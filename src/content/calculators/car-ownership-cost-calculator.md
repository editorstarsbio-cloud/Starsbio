## The Hidden Costs of Ownership

When most people buy a car, they only look at the sticker price and the loan payment. Financial experts recommend looking at the "Total Cost of Ownership" (TCO), which includes six major categories:

1. **Depreciation:** The largest hidden cost. A new car loses about 20% of its value in the first year and 15% each subsequent year.
2. **Auto Loan Interest:** Unless you pay cash, you are paying interest to a bank. On a 7-year loan, this can add thousands to the price.
3. **Insurance:** Varies wildly based on your age, driving record, and the car itself (sports cars cost significantly more to insure than minivans).
4. **Fuel:** Dependent on your annual mileage and the vehicle's MPG.
5. **Maintenance & Repairs:** Oil changes, tires, brakes, and unexpected breakdowns. Older cars have lower depreciation but much higher repair costs.
6. **Taxes & Registration:** State and local fees paid annually.

**Example:**
You buy a $30,000 car with a $500 monthly loan payment ($6,000/year).
- Depreciation: $4,000/year
- Insurance: $1,200/year
- Gas: $1,500/year
- Maintenance: $800/year
- Registration: $300/year

Your loan payment is $6,000 a year, but your **True Cost of Ownership is $13,800 a year** (or $1,150 a month).

## How Much Car Can You Afford?

A common rule of thumb in personal finance is the **20/4/10 Rule**:
- Put down at least **20%**.
- Finance the car for no more than **4 years** (48 months).
- Keep total monthly vehicle expenses (loan, insurance, gas) under **10%** of your gross monthly income.

If you make $5,000 a month before taxes, you should ideally spend no more than $500 a month on *all* car-related expenses combined.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is it cheaper to lease or buy?**
Leasing usually offers a lower monthly payment because you are only paying for the depreciation that occurs during the 3-year lease term, not the whole car. However, buying is almost always cheaper in the long run. Once a loan is paid off, you own an asset and have no monthly payment. When a lease ends, you own nothing and must start a new lease immediately.
