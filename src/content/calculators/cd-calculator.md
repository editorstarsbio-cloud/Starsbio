## About the Certificate of Deposit (CD) Calculator

A Certificate of Deposit (CD) is a highly secure financial product offered by banks and credit unions. You agree to lock your money away for a specific amount of time (the "term"), and in exchange, the bank guarantees you a fixed, premium interest rate. Our **CD Calculator** shows exactly how much guaranteed profit you will make.

---

## How a CD Works

When you open a standard savings account, the interest rate is "variable," meaning the bank can drop it to 0.01% tomorrow without asking you. 

When you open a CD, you lock in the APY (Annual Percentage Yield) for the entire duration of the term. Even if the Federal Reserve drops interest rates to zero the next day, the bank is legally obligated to keep paying you the high rate you agreed upon until the CD matures.

**Example:**
You put $20,000 into a 5-year CD at 4.5% APY.
- You cannot touch the money for 5 years without paying a harsh penalty.
- The bank guarantees your money will grow at exactly 4.5% a year.
- After 5 years, your money has grown to **$24,923.63**. (A guaranteed $4,923 profit).

## What is a CD Ladder?

The biggest downside of a CD is illiquidity—your money is trapped. To solve this, investors build a "CD Ladder."

Instead of putting $50,000 into one 5-year CD, you split it into five $10,000 CDs with staggered terms:
- $10,000 in a 1-year CD
- $10,000 in a 2-year CD
- $10,000 in a 3-year CD
- $10,000 in a 4-year CD
- $10,000 in a 5-year CD

When the 1-year CD matures, you take that money (plus the interest) and buy a new 5-year CD. You do this every year. The result? You get the high interest rates of a 5-year CD, but you have $10,000 becoming available (liquid) every single year.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What happens if I need my money early?**
If you withdraw money before the maturity date, the bank will charge you an Early Withdrawal Penalty. This is usually equal to a certain number of months of interest (e.g., 3 months of interest for a 1-year CD, or 6 months for a 5-year CD). In some cases, the penalty can eat into your original principal.

**Are CDs safe?**
Yes. In the United States, CDs issued by FDIC-insured banks or NCUA-insured credit unions are backed by the federal government up to $250,000 per depositor. Even if the bank goes completely bankrupt, the government will refund your money.
