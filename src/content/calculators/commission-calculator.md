## About the Commission Calculator

Our **Commission Calculator** computes the payout a salesperson earns based on the sales they generate. It supports flat-rate commissions, tiered commission structures, and base-plus-commission models used in real estate, auto sales, and B2B software sales.

---

## Common Commission Structures

### 1. Straight Commission (Flat Rate)
The simplest model: you earn a fixed percentage of total sales.
$$ \text{Commission} = \text{Total Sales} \times \text{Commission Rate} $$
**Example:** A real estate agent sells a $400,000 house with a 3% commission.
$$ \text{Commission} = 400,000 \times 0.03 = \$12,000 $$

### 2. Base Salary + Commission
The salesperson receives a guaranteed salary plus a percentage of sales.
$$ \text{Total Pay} = \text{Base Salary} + (\text{Total Sales} \times \text{Commission Rate}) $$

### 3. Tiered (Graduated) Commission
The commission rate increases as sales volume hits certain milestones, incentivizing top performers.
- 5% on the first $50,000 in sales
- 8% on sales from $50,001 to $100,000
- 12% on sales above $100,000

**Example:** You sell $120,000 in a month on the tiered plan above:
- Tier 1: $50,000 × 0.05 = $2,500
- Tier 2: $50,000 × 0.08 = $4,000
- Tier 3: $20,000 × 0.12 = $2,400
- **Total Commission = $8,900**

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is a draw against commission?**
A "draw" is an advance payment to a salesperson, guaranteeing them a minimum income for the month. If they earn more in commission than the draw, they get the difference. If they earn less, they owe the company the difference out of future commissions (a "recoverable draw").

**Are commission payments taxed differently?**
In the United States, commissions are treated as supplemental income. Employers may withhold taxes on commissions at a flat 22% rate, or add them to your regular pay and tax them at your normal bracket. However, your *actual* tax liability at year-end is the same regardless of how it was withheld.
