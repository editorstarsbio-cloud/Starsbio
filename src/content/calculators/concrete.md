## How Concrete Volume is Calculated

Concrete is ordered by volume. For a rectangular slab (the most common case), the formula is simply:

$$ \text{Volume} = \text{Length} \times \text{Width} \times \text{Thickness} $$

**Example:** A rectangular driveway 20 feet long, 10 feet wide, and 4 inches (0.333 feet) thick:
$$ V = 20 \times 10 \times 0.333 = 66.67 \text{ cubic feet} $$

To convert cubic feet to cubic yards (the unit concrete is sold in), divide by 27:
$$ 66.67 \div 27 \approx 2.47 \text{ cubic yards} $$

### The 10% Waste Rule
Always add **10% extra** to your calculated volume to account for spillage, uneven ground, and slight miscalculations:
$$ \text{Order} = 2.47 \times 1.10 \approx 2.72 \text{ cubic yards} $$

## Concrete Mix Ratios

Ready-mix concrete is ordered in cubic yards from a plant. For small DIY projects using pre-mixed bags (like Quikrete), use this conversion:
- One 80-lb bag fills approximately **0.60 cubic feet**
- For 66.67 cubic feet: $66.67 \div 0.60 \approx 111$ bags

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How thick should a concrete slab be?**
Standard residential driveways require 4 inches of thickness. Slabs supporting heavy vehicles (like RVs or commercial trucks) should be 6 inches. Walkways and patios are typically 3–4 inches.

**What is PSI in concrete?**
PSI (Pounds per Square Inch) measures concrete's compressive strength. Residential driveways and patios typically use **3,000 PSI** concrete. Structural foundations often require **4,000 PSI** or higher.

**Does the calculator include rebar?**
No. This calculator estimates concrete volume only. Reinforcement (rebar or wire mesh) is a separate quantity estimate. As a general rule, rebar is spaced every 12–18 inches in a grid pattern for structural slabs.
