## How Credit Card Interest Works

Credit cards use a **Daily Periodic Rate (DPR)** to calculate interest:

$$ \text{DPR} = \frac{\text{Annual Interest Rate}}{365} $$

Each day, your balance is multiplied by the DPR to determine that day's interest charge. At the end of your billing cycle, all these daily charges are added to your balance.

**Example:** A $5,000 balance at 24% APR:
$$ \text{DPR} = \frac{0.24}{365} = 0.0657\% \text{ per day} $$
$$ \text{Monthly Interest} \approx 5000 \times \frac{0.24}{12} = \$100 \text{ per month} $$

## The Minimum Payment Trap

Credit card companies set minimum payments low (often 1–2% of the balance or $25, whichever is higher) to maximize the total interest you pay.

**Scenario:** $5,000 at 24% APR, minimum payment of $100/month:
- **Payoff time:** Over 9 years!
- **Total interest paid:** ~$5,700 (more than the original debt!)

**Same debt at $300/month:**
- Payoff time: Under 2 years
- Total interest: ~$1,200

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does paying more than the minimum actually help?**
Dramatically. Every dollar over the minimum goes directly to reducing principal. A smaller principal means less interest is charged next month, which accelerates the payoff snowball.

**Should I consolidate credit card debt?**
A personal loan or balance transfer card at a lower interest rate can significantly reduce total interest cost. If the new rate is genuinely lower AND you can avoid adding new charges, consolidation is usually beneficial.
