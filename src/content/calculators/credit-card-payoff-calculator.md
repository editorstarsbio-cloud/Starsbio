## About the Credit Card Payoff Calculator

Credit card debt is one of the most toxic forms of debt due to its astronomically high interest rates, often exceeding 20% or 25%. Our **Credit Card Payoff Calculator** shows you exactly how long it will take to eliminate your balance and how much interest you will pay along the way.

## The Minimum Payment Trap

Credit card companies are legally required to put a "Minimum Payment Warning" on your statement, and for good reason. The minimum payment is mathematically designed to keep you in debt for as long as possible, maximizing the bank's profit.

Usually, the minimum payment is calculated as $15 or $25, OR 1% to 2% of the principal balance plus accrued interest—whichever is higher. Because the payment barely covers the interest generated that month, your actual balance shrinks at a microscopic pace.

**Example:** You have a $5,000 balance at 21% APR. 
- If you pay the minimum (around $100 initially, decreasing over time), it will take you **17 years** to pay off the card, and you will pay over **$6,000 in interest**—more than the original debt!
- If you pay a fixed **$200 a month**, you pay it off in **2.8 years** and pay only **$1,500 in interest**.

## How to Pay Off Credit Cards Faster

1. **Stop using the card.** You cannot dig yourself out of a hole while still digging. Switch entirely to a debit card or cash.
2. **Balance Transfer:** If you have decent credit, transfer the balance to a card offering 0% APR for 12-18 months. You will pay a 3% transfer fee upfront, but 100% of your monthly payments will go directly to the principal.
3. **The Avalanche Method:** If you have multiple cards, pay the minimum on all of them, and throw every extra dollar at the card with the highest interest rate.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How is credit card interest calculated?**
Credit cards calculate interest *daily* based on your Average Daily Balance. They take your APR (e.g., 24%), divide it by 365 to get the Daily Periodic Rate (0.065%), and multiply that by your balance every single day. This is why paying your bill early in the billing cycle actually saves you a tiny amount of money compared to paying it on the due date.

**Does carrying a balance help my credit score?**
This is a persistent myth. Carrying a balance does absolutely nothing to help your credit score; it only costs you interest. To build excellent credit, use the card for regular purchases and pay the statement balance in full, 100%, every single month.
