## About the Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) Calculator

**Customer Lifetime Value (CLV or LTV)** is one of the most important metrics in business. It estimates the total amount of money a customer is expected to spend with your business, or the total profit they will generate, during their entire relationship with you. Knowing your CLV tells you exactly how much you can afford to spend to acquire a new customer.

---

## The Formula

There are many ways to calculate CLV depending on your business model (e.g., retail vs. subscription). A standard, robust formula is:

$$ \text{CLV} = \text{Average Order Value (AOV)} \times \text{Purchase Frequency} \times \text{Customer Lifespan} $$

To find the **Profit CLV** (which is much more useful than Revenue CLV):
$$ \text{Profit CLV} = \text{CLV} \times \text{Profit Margin} $$

**Example (E-commerce):**
- A customer spends an average of $50 per order.
- They buy from you 4 times per year.
- They stay loyal to your brand for an average of 3 years.
- Your profit margin is 30%.

1. Total Revenue CLV = $50 × 4 × 3 = $600
2. Total Profit CLV = $600 × 0.30 = **$180**

This tells you that you can spend up to $179 to acquire a new customer and still be profitable over the long term.

## CLV to CAC Ratio

CLV is almost always paired with **CAC (Customer Acquisition Cost)**. The ratio between them determines business health.

$$ \text{Ratio} = \frac{\text{CLV}}{\text{CAC}} $$

- **1:1** - You are losing money (acquisition costs equal profit, leaving nothing for operating expenses).
- **3:1** - Considered the "magic number" for SaaS and many businesses. You make 3x what you spend.
- **5:1 or higher** - Highly profitable, but it might mean you are under-spending on marketing and growing too slowly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I increase my Customer Lifetime Value?**
You can increase CLV by targeting three levers:
1. Increase Average Order Value (upselling, cross-selling, bundling).
2. Increase Purchase Frequency (email marketing, loyalty programs, subscriptions).
3. Increase Customer Lifespan (excellent customer service, improving product quality to reduce churn).

**What is Churn Rate?**
Churn rate is the percentage of customers who stop doing business with you over a given period. In subscription businesses, Customer Lifespan is often calculated simply as $1 / \text{Churn Rate}$. If you lose 10% of customers a year, your average lifespan is 1 / 0.10 = 10 years.
