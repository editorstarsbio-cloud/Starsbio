## Snowball vs. Avalanche Method

If you have multiple debts, you should always make the minimum payment on all of them. The question is: where do you put your *extra* money?

### 1. The Debt Snowball (Dave Ramsey Method)
You order your debts from **smallest balance to largest balance**, completely ignoring the interest rates. You throw all extra money at the smallest debt until it is gone. Then you roll that payment into the next smallest debt.
- **Pros:** Massive psychological wins. Eliminating a small debt quickly gives you the motivation and momentum to stick to the plan.
- **Cons:** It is mathematically more expensive. You pay more total interest over time.

### 2. The Debt Avalanche (Mathematical Method)
You order your debts from **highest interest rate to lowest interest rate**, ignoring the total balances. You throw all extra money at the debt with the highest rate (usually a credit card).
- **Pros:** It saves you the absolute maximum amount of money in interest and gets you out of debt the fastest.
- **Cons:** If your highest-interest debt is a massive $30,000 loan, it might take you years to pay it off, leading to burnout and loss of motivation because you don't get quick "wins."

## The Math Behind Debt Eradication

Let's say you have a $10,000 credit card balance at 24% interest. 
Your minimum payment is $250. 
If you only pay the minimum:
- It will take you **7.5 years (90 months)** to pay it off.
- You will pay **$12,500 in pure interest** (total paid = $22,500).

If you tighten your budget and pay **$500 per month**:
- It will take you **2.1 years (26 months)** to pay it off.
- You will pay only **$2,800 in interest**.
- **You saved yourself $9,700 and 5 years of your life.**

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does consolidating my debt make sense?**
Debt consolidation (taking out one large personal loan at a lower interest rate to pay off multiple high-interest credit cards) is mathematically sound. However, it is dangerous if you have not fixed the spending habits that caused the debt. Many people consolidate their debt, only to max out their empty credit cards again, leaving them with double the debt.

**Should I stop investing while paying off debt?**
Most financial advisors recommend stopping all investments (except an employer 401k match) to focus intensely on paying off high-interest consumer debt (anything over 8%). You cannot reliably beat a 24% credit card interest rate in the stock market.
