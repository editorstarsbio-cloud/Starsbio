## About the Density Calculator

**Density** measures how much mass is packed into a given volume. It is one of the most fundamental properties in materials science, chemistry, fluid mechanics, and everyday life. Density tells you whether objects will float or sink, how heavy a material is for its size, and determines many industrial material choices.

---

## The Formula

$$ \rho = \frac{m}{V} $$

Where:
- **$\rho$** (rho) = Density (kg/m³ or g/cm³)
- **$m$** = Mass (kg or g)
- **$V$** = Volume (m³ or cm³)

**Example:** A rectangular gold bar 10 cm × 5 cm × 2 cm weighing 1,930 g:
- Volume = $10 \times 5 \times 2 = 100$ cm³
$$ \rho = \frac{1930}{100} = 19.3 \text{ g/cm}^3 $$
This confirms the material is gold (density = 19.32 g/cm³).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why does ice float on water?**
Ice is less dense than liquid water — a rare property. When water freezes, its molecules form an open hexagonal lattice structure that takes up MORE space per molecule than liquid water. Ice has a density of ~917 kg/m³ vs. 1,000 kg/m³ for liquid water, so it floats. This property is essential to life on Earth — frozen lakes insulate the liquid water beneath.

**How is density used to identify unknown materials?**
If you measure an object's mass (using a scale) and its volume (by water displacement in a graduated cylinder), you can calculate its density and compare it to known material densities to identify the substance.
