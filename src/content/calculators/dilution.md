## About the Dilution Calculator

The **Dilution Calculator** is an essential tool for chemistry labs, medical professionals, and everyday household tasks. It determines exactly how much solvent (usually water) you need to add to a highly concentrated stock solution to achieve a specific, weaker concentration.

---

## The $C_1V_1 = C_2V_2$ Equation

The universal mathematical formula for dilution is:

$$ C_1V_1 = C_2V_2 $$

Where:
- **$C_1$** = Concentration of the initial stock solution
- **$V_1$** = Volume of the initial stock solution you need to use
- **$C_2$** = Desired concentration of the final solution
- **$V_2$** = Desired total volume of the final solution

As long as you know three of these variables, you can easily solve for the fourth.

**Example (Chemistry Lab):**
You have a stock solution of 10M Hydrochloric Acid ($C_1$). You need to make 500 mL ($V_2$) of a 2M solution ($C_2$). How much of the 10M stock do you need ($V_1$)?

$$ 10M \times V_1 = 2M \times 500\text{mL} $$
$$ 10 \times V_1 = 1000 $$
$$ V_1 = \mathbf{100\text{mL}} $$

To make the solution, you would take 100mL of the concentrated acid, and add 400mL of water to reach the 500mL total volume.

## Serial Dilutions

In biology and microbiology, extremely low concentrations are often required (e.g., to count bacterial colonies). It is physically impossible to measure 0.0001 mL of a liquid accurately. Instead, scientists use **Serial Dilutions**.

This involves diluting a solution by a factor of 10, taking a sample of *that* new solution, and diluting it by a factor of 10 again, repeating the process down a line of test tubes. 
A 1:10 dilution followed by another 1:10 dilution results in a 1:100 final dilution, allowing you to achieve microscopic concentrations using easily measurable volumes (like 1 mL).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is it important to add acid to water, not water to acid?**
When diluting concentrated strong acids (like Sulfuric Acid), the reaction is highly exothermic (it generates a massive amount of heat). If you drop water into acid, the water instantly boils on the surface and violently splatters boiling acid in your face. If you slowly add acid to a large volume of water, the large volume of water safely absorbs and dissipates the heat.

**Does volume always stay perfectly additive?**
In general, yes. 100mL of water + 100mL of juice = 200mL of liquid. However, in specific chemical cases (like mixing water and ethanol), the molecules interlock perfectly, and 50mL of water + 50mL of ethanol actually results in about 96mL of total volume, not 100mL.
