## About the Dividend Yield Calculator

**Dividend Yield** is a financial ratio that shows how much a company pays out in dividends each year relative to its stock price. Our **Dividend Yield Calculator** helps income investors evaluate whether a stock provides a sufficient passive cash flow compared to its risk.

---

## The Formula

The formula is a simple percentage:

$$ \text{Dividend Yield} = \left( \frac{\text{Annual Dividend per Share}}{\text{Current Stock Price}} \right) \times 100 $$

**Example:**
A company pays a quarterly dividend of $0.50 per share. The stock is currently trading at $50.00.
1. Annual Dividend = $0.50 \times 4 = \$2.00
2. Dividend Yield = $(2.00 / 50.00) \times 100 = \mathbf{4.0\%}$

If you invest $10,000 in this stock, you will receive $400 a year in passive cash flow.

---

## The Dividend Yield Trap

A high dividend yield is not always a good thing. Because the yield formula divides by the stock price, **if a stock price crashes, the yield artificially spikes**.

**Example:**
A stock is trading at $100 and pays a $5 dividend (5% yield). Suddenly, the company announces a massive lawsuit, and the stock price crashes to $25. 
The dividend yield is now $5 / $25 = **20%**. 

An inexperienced investor might see a 20% yield and buy it, thinking they found a gold mine. In reality, the company is likely bankrupt and will cut the dividend to $0 next month. This is known as a **value trap**. Always investigate *why* a yield is abnormally high (usually anything over 6-8%).

---

## Growth vs. Yield

Investors usually have to choose between high yield and high growth:
1. **High Yield (Utilities, Telecom, REITs):** These mature companies have no fast way to grow, so they return their profits to shareholders as cash. You get high income (4-7%) but very little stock price appreciation.
2. **Low Yield / High Growth (Tech, Biotech):** These companies reinvest all their profits back into research and expansion. They pay little to no dividend (0-1%), but the stock price might double in a few years.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is a Dividend Aristocrat?**
A Dividend Aristocrat is a company in the S&P 500 that has not only paid a dividend but has actively *increased* its dividend payout every single year for at least 25 consecutive years. Examples include Johnson & Johnson, Coca-Cola, and Procter & Gamble.

**Do I have to pay taxes on dividends?**
Yes. In a standard brokerage account, dividends are taxable income in the year you receive them, even if you automatically reinvest them. "Qualified" dividends are taxed at the lower capital gains rate, while "ordinary" dividends are taxed at your normal income tax rate.
