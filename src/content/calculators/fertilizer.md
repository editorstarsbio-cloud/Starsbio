## About the Fertilizer Calculator

Applying the exact right amount of fertilizer is critical for both crop yield and environmental protection. Our **Fertilizer Calculator** helps you convert the nutrient recommendations from a soil test into the actual pounds of physical fertilizer bags you need to buy and apply.

---

## Understanding the N-P-K Ratio

Every bag of commercial fertilizer has three bold numbers on the front, representing the **N-P-K ratio**:
1. **N (Nitrogen):** Promotes leafy green growth.
2. **P (Phosphorus):** Promotes root development and blooming.
3. **K (Potassium):** Promotes overall plant health and disease resistance.

The numbers represent the **percentage by weight** of that nutrient in the bag.
A 50-pound bag of **10-20-10** fertilizer contains:
- 10% Nitrogen ($50 \times 0.10 = 5 \text{ lbs}$)
- 20% Phosphorus ($50 \times 0.20 = 10 \text{ lbs}$)
- 10% Potassium ($50 \times 0.10 = 5 \text{ lbs}$)
The remaining 30 pounds in the bag are inert fillers (like sand or limestone) to make it easier to spread evenly.

---

## The Calculation Formula

To figure out how much bulk fertilizer you need to achieve a specific nutrient target:

$$ \text{Fertilizer Needed} = \frac{\text{Nutrient Target}}{\text{Nutrient Percentage (as decimal)}} $$

**Example:**
Your soil test recommends adding **2 pounds of Nitrogen per 1,000 square feet** of lawn. You go to the store and find a bag of **20-5-5** fertilizer.

1. The Nitrogen percentage is 20% (0.20).
2. Fertilizer Needed = $2 / 0.20 = \mathbf{10 \text{ lbs}}$.
You need to apply 10 pounds of the physical product per 1,000 sq ft to hit your Nitrogen target.

*(Note: Applying those 10 lbs will also incidentally add 0.5 lbs of Phosphorus and 0.5 lbs of Potassium).*

## The Danger of Over-Fertilizing

Applying more fertilizer than the calculator recommends will not make your plants grow faster. In fact, it is highly detrimental:
- **Fertilizer Burn:** Synthetic fertilizers are made of mineral salts. Too much salt draws moisture *out* of the plant roots, causing the plant to turn brown and die (fertilizer burn).
- **Environmental Runoff:** Plants can only absorb so much. The excess nitrogen and phosphorus wash away in the rain, ending up in local rivers and lakes, causing massive, toxic algae blooms that kill fish.
