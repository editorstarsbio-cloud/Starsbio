## About the Flooring Calculator

Our **Flooring Calculator** estimates how many square feet (or square meters) of flooring material you need to purchase, with built-in waste factors for different flooring types. Buying the right amount avoids costly return trips and ensures matching dye lots.

---

## How to Calculate Flooring Quantity

**Step 1: Measure each room in feet**
For rectangular rooms: $\text{Area} = \text{Length} \times \text{Width}$

For L-shaped rooms: split into two rectangles, calculate each, sum them.

**Step 2: Add waste factor**
| Flooring Type | Waste Factor |
|:---|:---|
| Hardwood (straight lay) | +7–10% |
| Hardwood (diagonal lay) | +15% |
| Tile | +10–15% |
| Laminate | +10% |
| Carpet | +10% |

**Step 3: Convert to units sold**
Carpet is usually sold by the yard: $\text{Square Yards} = \text{Square Feet} \div 9$

**Example:** A 14 ft × 12 ft room with laminate flooring:
- Area = $14 \times 12 = 168$ sq ft
- With 10% waste: $168 \times 1.10 = 184.8$ sq ft → **Purchase 185 sq ft**

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why do I need extra flooring for diagonal installation?**
Diagonal installation requires cutting planks at 45° angles along the walls, producing significantly more waste than straight runs. Always add a minimum 15% for diagonal patterns.

**Should I buy extra tiles for future repairs?**
Yes. Keep 5–10% of your tile surplus. Tiles are often discontinued, and you may need matching tiles for future repairs. The dye lot will also match your original installation.
