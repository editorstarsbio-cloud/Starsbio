## About the Force Calculator

**Force** is the fundamental cause of all motion and deformation in the physical world. Newton's Second Law of Motion is one of the most important equations in classical physics, used everywhere from aerospace engineering to sports science.

---

## Newton's Second Law

$$ F = m \times a $$

Where:
- **$F$** = Force, measured in Newtons (N)
- **$m$** = Mass, measured in kilograms (kg)
- **$a$** = Acceleration, measured in meters per second squared (m/s²)

This can be rearranged to solve for any variable:
$$ m = \frac{F}{a} \qquad a = \frac{F}{m} $$

**Example:** What force is needed to accelerate a 1,500 kg car from 0 to 60 km/h in 5 seconds?

First, find acceleration: $a = \frac{60 \text{ km/h}}{5 \text{ s}} = \frac{16.67 \text{ m/s}}{5} = 3.33 \text{ m/s}^2$

Then: $F = 1500 \times 3.33 = 4,995 \text{ N} \approx 5 \text{ kN}$

## Types of Force

| Type | Description |
|:---|:---|
| Gravity | $F_g = mg$ (where $g = 9.81$ m/s²) |
| Normal Force | Perpendicular surface reaction force |
| Friction | $F_f = \mu \times F_N$ (opposes motion) |
| Tension | Force through a rope or cable |
| Spring Force | $F = -kx$ (Hooke's Law) |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the difference between mass and weight?**
Mass ($m$) is the amount of matter in an object, measured in kg — it is constant everywhere in the universe. Weight ($W$) is the gravitational force on that mass: $W = mg$. On the Moon (where $g = 1.62$ m/s²), a 70 kg person weighs only 113 N, but their mass is still 70 kg.

**What is a Newton in everyday terms?**
1 Newton is approximately the force needed to hold a 100-gram apple against gravity ($0.1 \text{ kg} \times 9.81 \approx 1 \text{ N}$).
