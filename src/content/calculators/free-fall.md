## About the Free Fall Calculator

**Free fall** describes the motion of an object falling solely under the influence of gravity, with no air resistance. It is one of the most important problems in introductory physics, illustrating key concepts of constant acceleration, velocity, and distance.

---

## Key Equations of Free Fall

Near Earth's surface, gravity provides a constant downward acceleration of $g = 9.81$ m/s².

**Velocity at time $t$:**
$$ v = g \times t $$

**Distance fallen in time $t$:**
$$ d = \frac{1}{2} g t^2 $$

**Velocity after falling distance $d$:**
$$ v = \sqrt{2gd} $$

**Example:** A ball is dropped from a 100 m building. How long does it take to hit the ground and how fast is it going?

$$ t = \sqrt{\frac{2d}{g}} = \sqrt{\frac{200}{9.81}} = \sqrt{20.39} \approx 4.52 \text{ seconds} $$
$$ v = 9.81 \times 4.52 \approx 44.3 \text{ m/s} \approx 159 \text{ km/h} $$

---

## Terminal Velocity

In reality, air resistance opposes gravity. As speed increases, air resistance grows until it equals the gravitational force. The object then stops accelerating — it has reached **terminal velocity**.

Terminal velocity for a skydiver is approximately **53 m/s (190 km/h)** in a belly-down position.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do all objects fall at the same speed?**
In a vacuum (no air), yes — this was proven by Galileo. On Earth with air resistance, heavier, more aerodynamic objects fall faster. A feather falls slower than a bowling ball because its large surface area relative to its mass creates enormous drag.

**What is the acceleration of gravity on other planets?**

| Body | g (m/s²) |
|:---|:---|
| Moon | 1.62 |
| Mars | 3.72 |
| Earth | 9.81 |
| Jupiter | 24.79 |
