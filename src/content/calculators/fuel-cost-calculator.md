## The Formula

To calculate the total cost of fuel, you need three variables: Distance, Fuel Efficiency, and Price per unit of fuel.

**In MPG (US):**
$$ \text{Total Cost} = \left(\frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{MPG}}\right) \times \text{Price per Gallon} $$

**In L/100km (Metric):**
$$ \text{Total Cost} = \left(\frac{\text{Distance}}{100} \times \text{L/100km}\right) \times \text{Price per Liter} $$

**Example (US):** A 500-mile road trip in a car that gets 25 MPG, with gas at $3.50 per gallon:
$$ \text{Gallons needed} = \frac{500}{25} = 20 \text{ gallons} $$
$$ \text{Total Cost} = 20 \times 3.50 = \$70.00 $$

**Example (Metric):** An 800 km trip in a car that consumes 7.5 L/100km, with fuel at €1.60 per liter:
$$ \text{Liters needed} = \frac{800}{100} \times 7.5 = 60 \text{ liters} $$
$$ \text{Total Cost} = 60 \times 1.60 = €96.00 $$

## How to Improve Fuel Efficiency

1. **Check Tire Pressure:** Under-inflated tires increase rolling resistance and can lower gas mileage by 0.2% for every 1 psi drop.
2. **Avoid Aggressive Driving:** Speeding, rapid acceleration, and harsh braking can lower gas mileage by 15% to 30% at highway speeds.
3. **Use Cruise Control:** Maintaining a constant speed on the highway prevents unnecessary acceleration.
4. **Remove Excess Weight:** An extra 100 pounds in your vehicle could reduce your MPG by about 1%.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does running the AC use more gas?**
Yes, running the air conditioning can reduce your fuel economy by more than 25%, particularly on short trips. However, driving on the highway with windows down increases aerodynamic drag, which also burns fuel. Above 50 mph, using AC is generally more efficient than opening windows.

**How accurate is the estimated MPG on my car's dashboard?**
Dashboard MPG estimates can be off by 1-2 MPG. For the most accurate calculation, divide the actual miles driven (from your trip odometer) by the exact number of gallons you put in the tank at the pump.
