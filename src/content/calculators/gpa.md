## About the GPA Calculator

Your **Grade Point Average (GPA)** is a single standardized number representing your overall academic performance. Colleges, graduate schools, scholarship committees, and employers use it to quickly evaluate academic achievement across different institutions.

---

## How GPA is Calculated

In the United States, most schools use a **4.0 scale** where letter grades correspond to specific grade points:

| Letter Grade | Percentage | Grade Points |
|:---|:---|:---|
| A+ / A | 93–100% | 4.0 |
| A− | 90–92% | 3.7 |
| B+ | 87–89% | 3.3 |
| B | 83–86% | 3.0 |
| B− | 80–82% | 2.7 |
| C+ | 77–79% | 2.3 |
| C | 73–76% | 2.0 |
| D | 60–69% | 1.0 |
| F | Below 60% | 0.0 |

### Weighted GPA Formula

GPA is calculated by multiplying each course's grade points by its credit hours (weight), summing them, then dividing by total credit hours:

$$ \text{GPA} = \frac{\sum (\text{Grade Points} \times \text{Credit Hours})}{\sum \text{Credit Hours}} $$

**Example:**

| Course | Grade | Credits | Points |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| English | A (4.0) | 3 | 12.0 |
| Math | B+ (3.3) | 4 | 13.2 |
| History | B (3.0) | 3 | 9.0 |

$$ \text{GPA} = \frac{12.0 + 13.2 + 9.0}{3 + 4 + 3} = \frac{34.2}{10} = 3.42 $$

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What GPA is required for college admission?**
Selective universities typically expect a GPA of 3.7 or higher. State universities often accept students with a 3.0 GPA. Community colleges generally accept all applicants regardless of GPA.

**What is the difference between weighted and unweighted GPA?**
An unweighted GPA treats all courses equally on the 4.0 scale. A weighted GPA awards extra points for Advanced Placement (AP) or Honors courses — an A in AP Chemistry might count as 5.0 instead of 4.0, rewarding academic rigor.
