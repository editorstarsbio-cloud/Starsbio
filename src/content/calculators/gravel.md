## About the Gravel Calculator

Whether you are laying a driveway, filling a patio base, or creating landscaping beds, buying the right amount of gravel is crucial. Buy too little, and you have to pay for a second delivery. Buy too much, and you have a giant pile of rocks killing your grass. Our **Gravel Calculator** determines exactly how much material you need.

## The Volume Formula

Gravel is sold by volume (cubic yards or cubic meters) or by weight (tons). First, you must calculate the cubic volume of your project space.

$$ \text{Volume} = \text{Length} \times \text{Width} \times \text{Depth} $$

*Note: You must convert all measurements to the same unit before multiplying. Usually, length and width are measured in feet, while depth is measured in inches. Convert the depth to feet by dividing by 12.*

**Example:**
You want to build a gravel driveway that is 40 feet long, 10 feet wide, and needs to be 4 inches deep.
1. Convert depth: $4 \text{ inches} / 12 = 0.33 \text{ feet}$.
2. Calculate Cubic Feet: $40 \times 10 \times 0.33 = 132 \text{ cubic feet}$.

---

## Converting to Cubic Yards and Tons

Suppliers do not sell gravel in cubic feet; they sell it in **cubic yards**. 
There are 27 cubic feet in a cubic yard ($3 \text{ ft} \times 3 \text{ ft} \times 3 \text{ ft}$).

$$ \text{Cubic Yards} = \frac{\text{Cubic Feet}}{27} $$

In the example above: $132 / 27 = \mathbf{4.89 \text{ cubic yards}}$. You would likely order 5 cubic yards.

If your supplier sells by the ton, the general rule of thumb is that **1 cubic yard of standard gravel weighs roughly 1.4 tons** (2,800 lbs).
$4.89 \text{ cubic yards} \times 1.4 = \mathbf{6.85 \text{ tons}}$.

---

## Compaction Factor

When gravel is dumped from a truck, it is loose. When you run a plate compactor over it, it compresses. For a solid driveway or patio base, you should add a **10% to 15% compaction factor** to your final number to ensure you don't run short after tamping the gravel down.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How deep should my gravel be?**
- Driveways: 4 to 8 inches (depending on the weight of vehicles).
- Pathways and Patios: 2 to 4 inches.
- Decorative Landscaping Beds: 1 to 2 inches (laid over a weed barrier).

**What is "Crush and Run"?**
Crush and Run (or Crusher Run) is a mix of angular crushed stone and stone dust. It is specifically used for driveways and bases because the dust fills the voids between the rocks, allowing it to compact into a nearly solid, concrete-like surface.
