## About the House Affordability Calculator

Our **House Affordability Calculator** estimates the maximum home price you can realistically afford based on your income, debts, down payment, and current interest rates. It applies the same debt-to-income standards that mortgage lenders use during underwriting.

## The 28/36 Rule

The most widely used affordability guideline in mortgage lending is the **28/36 Rule**:

- **28% Rule:** Your total monthly housing costs (mortgage principal, interest, taxes, insurance) should not exceed 28% of your gross monthly income.
- **36% Rule:** Your total monthly debt payments (housing + car loans + student loans + credit cards) should not exceed 36% of your gross monthly income.

**Example:** Gross monthly income = $7,000

- Maximum housing payment: $7,000 × 0.28 = **$1,960/month**
- Maximum total debt: $7,000 × 0.36 = $2,520/month

If you have $500/month in other debts, your max housing payment under the 36% rule drops to $2,520 − $500 = **$2,020/month**.

## From Payment to Home Price

Using the mortgage payment formula, work backwards from the maximum payment to find the maximum loan amount, then add your down payment:

$$ P = M \times \frac{(1+r)^n - 1}{r(1+r)^n} $$

At 7% interest over 30 years, a $1,960/month payment supports a loan of approximately **$295,000**. With a 20% down payment, you could afford a home priced at **$368,750**.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What credit score do I need to buy a house?**
For a conventional loan: typically 620+ minimum, with best rates at 740+. FHA loans allow scores as low as 580 with 3.5% down, or even 500 with 10% down.

**How much down payment do I need?**
Conventional loans typically require 5–20%. FHA loans require 3.5%. VA and USDA loans offer 0% down for eligible buyers. Putting less than 20% down usually requires paying Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI).
