## About the Ideal Weight Calculator

Our **Ideal Weight Calculator** estimates the weight range associated with the lowest risk of weight-related health conditions for a given height. It does not define beauty standards — it reflects statistical population health data.

> **Medical Disclaimer:** Ideal weight formulas are statistical estimates, not personal medical targets. Individual factors like bone density, muscle mass, and genetics vary widely. Always consult a physician before setting weight goals.

## Common Ideal Weight Formulas

Several different formulas have been developed by physicians and researchers over the decades:

**Hamwi Formula (1964):**
- Men: 48 kg + 2.7 kg per inch over 5 feet
- Women: 45.5 kg + 2.2 kg per inch over 5 feet

**Robinson Formula (1983):**
- Men: 52 kg + 1.9 kg per inch over 5 feet
- Women: 49 kg + 1.7 kg per inch over 5 feet

**BMI-Based Range (WHO Standard):**
$$ \text{Weight range} = \text{BMI} \times \text{height}^2 \quad \text{(height in meters)} $$
For a healthy BMI of 18.5–24.9.

**Example (Hamwi, Male, 5'10"):**
5'10" = 10 inches over 5 feet:
$$ \text{Ideal Weight} = 48 + (2.7 \times 10) = 48 + 27 = 75 \text{ kg} $$

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do these formulas work for athletes?**
No. All ideal weight formulas assume average body composition. Athletes, particularly weightlifters and rugby players, carry significantly more muscle mass. For them, body fat percentage is a far more relevant metric than weight alone.

**Which formula is most accurate?**
No single formula is universally "best." The Miller formula tends to be slightly more accurate for taller individuals; the Hamwi formula is widely used in clinical settings. Using a healthy BMI range (18.5–24.9) to derive a weight range is arguably the most practical approach.
