## How Inflation is Calculated

Inflation is measured using price indices, most commonly the **Consumer Price Index (CPI)** in the United States. The CPI tracks the average price change of a basket of goods and services over time.

To convert a past price to its present value:

$$ \text{Present Value} = \text{Past Value} \times \frac{\text{CPI Today}}{\text{CPI in Past Year}} $$

Or, using an average annual inflation rate:

$$ \text{Future Value} = \text{Present Value} \times (1 + r)^n $$

**Example:** What did $100 in 1990 cost in 2024, assuming 2.8% average annual inflation?
$$ FV = 100 \times (1.028)^{34} = 100 \times 2.567 = \$256.70 $$

$100 in 1990 had the same buying power as **$256.70 in 2024**.

## Why Inflation Matters for Investing

A savings account earning 2% interest during a 4% inflation period has a **real return of −2%**. You are losing purchasing power even as your account balance grows.

$$ \text{Real Return} \approx \text{Nominal Return} - \text{Inflation Rate} $$

This is why financial advisors recommend investments that outpace inflation long-term.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is hyperinflation?**
Hyperinflation occurs when prices rise at more than 50% per month. Historical examples include Zimbabwe (2007–2008, prices doubling every 24 hours) and Weimar Germany (1921–1923). It is typically caused by excessive money printing and total loss of confidence in a currency.

**What is the Fed's inflation target?**
The US Federal Reserve officially targets **2% annual inflation** as the optimal rate — high enough to prevent deflation (falling prices, which cause consumers to delay purchases) but low enough to protect purchasing power.
