## About the Inventory Turnover Calculator

**Inventory Turnover** is a financial ratio showing how many times a company has sold and replaced inventory during a given period. It is a critical metric for retail and manufacturing businesses, indicating how efficiently they are managing stock and generating sales.

---

## The Formula

The standard formula for inventory turnover is:

$$ \text{Inventory Turnover} = \frac{\text{Cost of Goods Sold (COGS)}}{\text{Average Inventory}} $$

Where:
$$ \text{Average Inventory} = \frac{\text{Beginning Inventory} + \text{Ending Inventory}}{2} $$

**Example:**
A retail store has a Beginning Inventory of $50,000 and an Ending Inventory of $70,000. Their Cost of Goods Sold for the year is $360,000.

1. Average Inventory = $(50,000 + 70,000) / 2 = \$60,000$
2. Inventory Turnover = $360,000 / 60,000 = \mathbf{6 \text{ times}}$

This means the store sold out and restocked its entire inventory equivalent 6 times during the year.

---

## Days Sales of Inventory (DSI)

To translate the turnover ratio into something more intuitive, businesses calculate how many days it takes to clear the inventory:

$$ \text{Days in Inventory} = \frac{365}{\text{Inventory Turnover Ratio}} $$

In the example above: $365 / 6 = \mathbf{60.8 \text{ days}}$. The store holds a product for about two months before it sells.

## What Does Your Ratio Mean?

| Ratio | Implication |
|:---|:---|
| **High Turnover (e.g., 10+)** | Strong sales or inadequate inventory levels. Fast-moving consumer goods (FMCG) naturally have high ratios. |
| **Low Turnover (e.g., 2)** | Poor sales, excess inventory, or dead stock. High-ticket items (cars, jewelry) naturally have lower ratios. |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is a higher inventory turnover always better?**
Generally, yes, because holding inventory costs money (storage, insurance, obsolescence). However, an abnormally high ratio might indicate inadequate inventory levels, leading to stockouts and lost sales because customers cannot find what they want to buy.

**Why use COGS instead of Sales Revenue?**
Sales revenue includes the markup (profit). Inventory is recorded on the balance sheet at cost. If you divide sales (which includes markup) by inventory (which does not), you artificially inflate the turnover ratio. Dividing cost by cost ensures an accurate metric.
