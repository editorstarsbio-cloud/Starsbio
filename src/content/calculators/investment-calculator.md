## The Core Formula: Future Value

$$ FV = PV \times (1 + r)^n $$

Where:
- **$FV$** = Future value of the investment
- **$PV$** = Present value (initial investment)
- **$r$** = Annual rate of return (as a decimal)
- **$n$** = Number of years

**Example:** $10,000 invested at 8% average annual return for 20 years:
$$ FV = 10,000 \times (1.08)^{20} = 10,000 \times 4.661 = \$46,610 $$

Your $10,000 grows to nearly **$46,610** — 3.66× your original investment, with the majority of growth coming from compounding in the later years.

## Adding Regular Contributions

If you also add $200 per month:

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What return rate should I use?**
For long-term equity (stock market) investments, many financial planners use 7% as a conservative inflation-adjusted estimate. For bonds, 2–3%. For a 60/40 portfolio, around 5–6%.

**How does inflation affect investment returns?**
To find your real return after inflation, use the formula:
$$ \text{Real Return} = \frac{1 + \text{Nominal Rate}}{1 + \text{Inflation Rate}} - 1 $$
At 8% nominal return and 3% inflation: $\frac{1.08}{1.03} - 1 \approx 4.85\%$ real return.
