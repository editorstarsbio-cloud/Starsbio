## About the Internal Rate of Return (IRR) Calculator

The **Internal Rate of Return (IRR)** is a metric used in financial analysis to estimate the profitability of potential investments. It is the annual rate of growth that an investment is expected to generate. 

Specifically, IRR is the discount rate that makes the Net Present Value (NPV) of all cash flows from a particular project equal to zero. 

---

## How IRR Works

Unlike NPV, which outputs a dollar amount, IRR outputs a percentage. This makes it highly intuitive for comparing different investments.

The formula involves setting the NPV equation to zero and solving for the discount rate ($r$):

$$ 0 = \sum_{t=1}^{n} \frac{C_t}{(1 + r)^t} - C_0 $$

Where:
- $C_t$ = Net cash inflow during the period $t$
- $C_0$ = Total initial investment costs
- $r$ = The Internal Rate of Return (IRR)
- $t$ = The number of time periods

Because $r$ is in the denominator of a summation, the IRR equation cannot easily be solved algebraically; it is found through trial and error or financial software calculators.

---

## The Golden Rule of IRR

Companies have a "hurdle rate," which is the minimum acceptable return on an investment (often based on their cost of capital).
- **If IRR > Hurdle Rate:** The project is profitable and should be **accepted**.
- **If IRR < Hurdle Rate:** The project will destroy value and should be **rejected**.

**Example:**
A company evaluates two projects. Project A has an IRR of 14%. Project B has an IRR of 8%. The company's hurdle rate is 10%. They will accept Project A and reject Project B.

---

## Limitations of IRR

1. **Reinvestment Assumption:** IRR assumes that future cash flows generated by the project are reinvested at the IRR rate itself. This is often unrealistic (a project might have a 40% IRR, but you can't realistically reinvest the cash it generates at 40%). The Modified Internal Rate of Return (MIRR) fixes this.
2. **Multiple IRRs:** If a project has alternating cash flows (e.g., negative, positive, negative), the math can produce multiple valid IRRs, making the metric useless.
3. **Scale Ignorance:** A $100 investment with a 50% IRR generates $50 of value. A $10,000 investment with a 15% IRR generates $1,500 of value. IRR would choose the $100 project, even though the $10,000 project creates more absolute wealth.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is a "good" IRR?**
A good IRR depends entirely on the investor's cost of capital and risk tolerance. For a safe corporate project, 12% might be excellent. For a high-risk venture capital investment, an IRR of 30-40% might be required to justify the risk.

**IRR vs. ROI: What is the difference?**
ROI simply measures the total percentage return from start to finish, completely ignoring the time value of money. An ROI of 50% over 1 year is great; an ROI of 50% over 10 years is poor. IRR factors in time and compounding, providing an annualized rate of return.
