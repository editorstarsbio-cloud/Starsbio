## About the Kinetic Energy Calculator

**Kinetic energy** is the energy an object possesses due to its motion. The faster an object moves, or the more massive it is, the more kinetic energy it carries. This energy is what makes a moving car dangerous, a bullet lethal, and wind powerful enough to spin turbines.

---

## The Formula

$$ KE = \frac{1}{2} m v^2 $$

Where:
- **$KE$** = Kinetic Energy, measured in Joules (J)
- **$m$** = Mass, in kilograms (kg)
- **$v$** = Velocity, in meters per second (m/s)

**Example 1 — Car crash:** A 1,500 kg car traveling at 60 km/h (16.67 m/s):
$$ KE = \frac{1}{2} \times 1500 \times 16.67^2 = \frac{1}{2} \times 1500 \times 277.89 = 208,418 \text{ J} = 208.4 \text{ kJ} $$

**Example 2 — Speed doubles:** If the car goes at 120 km/h (doubled speed):
$$ KE = \frac{1}{2} \times 1500 \times 33.33^2 = 833,267 \text{ J} = 833.3 \text{ kJ} $$

Doubling speed quadruples kinetic energy! This is why speed limits matter so much in crash safety.

---

## Work-Energy Theorem

The work done on an object equals its change in kinetic energy:
$$ W = \Delta KE = KE_{\text{final}} - KE_{\text{initial}} $$

This is why braking distances increase quadratically with speed, not linearly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does kinetic energy depend on direction?**
No. Kinetic energy is a **scalar** quantity (it has magnitude but no direction). A car going north at 60 mph and a car going south at 60 mph have identical kinetic energies, even though their velocities (vectors) are opposite.

**What happens to kinetic energy in a perfectly elastic collision?**
In a perfectly elastic collision, total kinetic energy is conserved — no energy is lost to heat or deformation. In real-world collisions, some kinetic energy is always converted to heat, sound, and deformation.
