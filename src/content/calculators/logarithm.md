## About the Logarithm Calculator

A **logarithm** answers the question: "To what power must I raise a base to get a certain number?" Logarithms are the inverse of exponentiation and appear throughout science, engineering, music theory, and computer science.

---

## The Core Definition

If $b^x = y$, then $\log_b(y) = x$

In words: "The logarithm base $b$ of $y$ is $x$" means "b raised to the power $x$ gives $y$."

**Example:** $10^3 = 1000$, therefore $\log_{10}(1000) = 3$

---

## Types of Logarithms

### Common Logarithm (Base 10) — written as $\log$
$$ \log(1000) = 3, \quad \log(100) = 2, \quad \log(10) = 1, \quad \log(1) = 0 $$

Used in: pH chemistry, Richter scale, decibels (dB), finance.

### Natural Logarithm (Base e) — written as $\ln$
$$ e \approx 2.71828 $$
$$ \ln(e^2) = 2, \quad \ln(1) = 0 $$

Used in: continuous compound interest, population growth, radioactive decay.

### Binary Logarithm (Base 2) — written as $\log_2$
$$ \log_2(8) = 3, \quad \log_2(1024) = 10 $$

Used in: computer science (information theory, complexity analysis).

---

## Key Logarithm Rules

$$ \log(ab) = \log(a) + \log(b) $$
$$ \log\left(\frac{a}{b}\right) = \log(a) - \log(b) $$
$$ \log(a^n) = n \cdot \log(a) $$

**Change of Base Formula** (to calculate any base using a calculator):
$$ \log_b(x) = \frac{\ln(x)}{\ln(b)} = \frac{\log(x)}{\log(b)} $$

---

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why can't you take the logarithm of a negative number?**
There is no real power you can raise a positive base to in order to get a negative result. The logarithm of zero or a negative number is undefined in the real number system.

**What is the natural log of 1?**
$\ln(1) = 0$, because $e^0 = 1$. This is true for any base: $\log_b(1) = 0$.
