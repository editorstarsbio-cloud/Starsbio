## About the Markup Calculator

**Markup** is the percentage added to the cost price of a product to determine its selling price. It is how businesses ensure they cover costs and generate profit on every sale. Markup is expressed as a percentage of the **cost**, while margin is expressed as a percentage of the **revenue** — this critical distinction trips up many small business owners.

---

## The Markup Formula

$$ \text{Markup \%} = \frac{\text{Selling Price} - \text{Cost}}{\text{Cost}} \times 100 $$

$$ \text{Selling Price} = \text{Cost} \times \left(1 + \frac{\text{Markup \%}}{100}\right) $$

**Example:** You manufacture a product for $40 and want a 75% markup:
$$ \text{Selling Price} = 40 \times (1 + 0.75) = 40 \times 1.75 = \$70 $$

---

## Markup vs. Margin — The Critical Difference

| Metric | Base | Formula | Example (Cost $40, Price $70) |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **Markup** | Cost | (Price − Cost) / Cost | ($70−$40)/$40 = **75%** |
| **Margin** | Revenue | (Price − Cost) / Price | ($70−$40)/$70 = **42.9%** |

A 75% markup equals a 42.9% margin. Confusing these leads to severely underpriced products.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What markup do I need to achieve a specific profit margin?**
$$ \text{Required Markup} = \frac{\text{Desired Margin}}{1 - \text{Desired Margin}} \times 100 $$
To achieve a 40% margin: Markup = $\frac{0.40}{0.60} \times 100 = 66.7\%$

**Can markup exceed 100%?**
Yes. A 100% markup means you doubled the cost. Jewelry, fashion, and technology products regularly carry markups of 200–500%.
