## About the Molar Mass Calculator

**Molar mass** is the mass of one mole of a substance, measured in grams per mole (g/mol). It is numerically equal to the average atomic mass of the substance's atoms or molecules. Calculating molar mass is the essential first step in nearly every quantitative chemistry problem.

---

## How to Calculate Molar Mass

Simply add up the atomic masses of all atoms in the chemical formula, using the periodic table.

**Example 1 — Water (H₂O):**
- Hydrogen (H): 1.008 g/mol × 2 = 2.016 g/mol
- Oxygen (O): 16.00 g/mol × 1 = 16.00 g/mol
- **Molar Mass of H₂O = 18.016 g/mol**

**Example 2 — Glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆):**
- Carbon: 12.011 × 6 = 72.066
- Hydrogen: 1.008 × 12 = 12.096
- Oxygen: 15.999 × 6 = 95.994
- **Molar Mass of C₆H₁₂O₆ = 180.156 g/mol**

---

## Moles ↔ Grams Conversion

$$ \text{Moles} = \frac{\text{Mass (g)}}{\text{Molar Mass (g/mol)}} $$
$$ \text{Mass (g)} = \text{Moles} \times \text{Molar Mass (g/mol)} $$

**Example:** How many moles are in 50 grams of NaCl (Molar mass = 58.44 g/mol)?
$$ n = \frac{50}{58.44} = 0.856 \text{ mol} $$

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the difference between molar mass and atomic mass?**
Atomic mass refers to a single atom and is expressed in atomic mass units (amu or u). Molar mass refers to one mole ($6.022 \times 10^{23}$ particles) and is expressed in g/mol. Numerically, they are equal — carbon-12 has an atomic mass of 12 amu and a molar mass of 12 g/mol.

**What is Avogadro's number?**
$N_A = 6.022 \times 10^{23}$ — the number of particles (atoms, molecules, ions) in one mole of substance. One mole of water contains $6.022 \times 10^{23}$ water molecules.
