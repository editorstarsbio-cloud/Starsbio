## About the Molarity Calculator

**Molarity** is the most widely used unit of concentration in chemistry. It describes how many moles of a solute are dissolved in one liter of solution. Our **Molarity Calculator** is essential for students in chemistry labs, pharmacists preparing solutions, and scientists formulating compounds.

---

## The Formula

$$ M = \frac{n}{V} $$

Where:
- **$M$** = Molarity, measured in mol/L (molar)
- **$n$** = Number of moles of solute
- **$V$** = Volume of solution in liters

And since **Moles = Mass ÷ Molar Mass**:

$$ M = \frac{\text{mass (g)}}{\text{molar mass (g/mol)} \times \text{volume (L)}} $$

**Example:** Dissolve 5.85 g of sodium chloride (NaCl, molar mass = 58.44 g/mol) in 250 mL (0.25 L) of water:
$$ n = \frac{5.85}{58.44} = 0.1 \text{ mol} $$
$$ M = \frac{0.1}{0.25} = 0.4 \text{ mol/L} $$

The solution has a molarity of **0.4 M**.

---

## Dilution Formula

When you dilute a concentrated solution, the number of moles stays constant:

$$ M_1 V_1 = M_2 V_2 $$

**Example:** How much water must you add to 100 mL of a 2.0 M solution to make a 0.5 M solution?
$$ V_2 = \frac{M_1 V_1}{M_2} = \frac{2.0 \times 100}{0.5} = 400 \text{ mL total volume} $$
You need to add **300 mL** of water.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the difference between molarity and molality?**
Molarity ($M$) is moles per **liter of solution** (the total volume). Molality ($m$) is moles per **kilogram of solvent** (only the dissolving liquid). Molality is used in thermodynamics (for boiling/freezing point calculations) because it does not change with temperature the way volume does.

**What is normality?**
Normality ($N$) is an older concentration unit equal to molarity multiplied by the number of reactive units (equivalents) per molecule. For acids and bases, it is still used in titration calculations.
