## About the Momentum Calculator

**Momentum** is the quantity of motion an object has. It is directly proportional to both its mass and velocity. Understanding momentum is critical for analyzing collisions, explosions, and the transfer of force in physics and engineering.

---

## The Formula

$$ p = m \times v $$

Where:
- **$p$** = Momentum (kg·m/s)
- **$m$** = Mass (kg)
- **$v$** = Velocity (m/s)

**Example:** A 1,500 kg car traveling at 20 m/s (72 km/h):
$$ p = 1500 \times 20 = 30,000 \text{ kg·m/s} $$

---

## Conservation of Momentum

The **Law of Conservation of Momentum** states that in a closed system with no external forces, total momentum before and after a collision is equal:

$$ m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2 = m_1 v_1' + m_2 v_2' $$

**Elastic Collision Example:** A 2 kg ball moving at 5 m/s hits a stationary 3 kg ball. After impact, the 2 kg ball stops. How fast does the 3 kg ball move?
$$ (2)(5) + (3)(0) = (2)(0) + (3)v_2' $$
$$ 10 = 3v_2' \Rightarrow v_2' = 3.33 \text{ m/s} $$

## Impulse and Momentum

Impulse ($J$) is the change in momentum caused by a force over time:
$$ J = F \times \Delta t = \Delta p = m \Delta v $$

This explains why airbags save lives: they extend the collision time $\Delta t$, which reduces the peak force $F$ on the passenger even though the change in momentum remains the same.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does momentum have direction?**
Yes. Momentum is a vector quantity — it has both magnitude and direction. Two objects of equal mass moving at equal speeds in opposite directions have a combined momentum of zero.

**What is angular momentum?**
Angular momentum ($L = I\omega$) is the rotational equivalent of linear momentum, used for spinning objects, planets, and gyroscopes. It is also conserved when no external torque acts on the system.
