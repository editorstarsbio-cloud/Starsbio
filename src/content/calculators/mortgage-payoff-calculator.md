A 30-year mortgage costs a fortune in interest. Our **Mortgage Payoff Calculator** shows you exactly how much time and money you can save by making extra payments toward your principal balance.

## The Power of Extra Principal Payments

Because mortgages are amortized, the vast majority of your payment in the first 10 years goes entirely toward interest, not the principal (the actual amount you borrowed). 

When you make an *extra* payment, 100% of it goes directly to the principal. This immediately reduces the balance upon which next month's interest is calculated, creating a compounding effect of savings.

**Example:** 
You have a $300,000 mortgage at 6.5% interest over 30 years. Your standard principal & interest payment is $1,896/month. If you pay it normally, you will pay **$382,633 in total interest** over 30 years.

If you add just **$200 extra per month**:
- You will pay off the loan in **23 years and 4 months** (saving 6+ years).
- You will pay only **$278,930 in total interest**.
- That $200/month investment saves you **$103,703** in interest.

## Strategies for Paying Off a Mortgage Early

1. **Bi-Weekly Payments:** Instead of one payment a month, pay half your mortgage every two weeks. Because there are 52 weeks in a year, you will make 26 half-payments, which equals 13 full payments. This effortless strategy automatically adds one extra principal payment per year.
2. **The "Round Up" Method:** If your mortgage payment is $1,432, round it up to $1,500. You likely won't miss the $68, but it shaves years off the loan.
3. **Lump Sum Injections:** Apply annual work bonuses, tax refunds, or inheritance money directly to the principal.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is it always a good idea to pay off a mortgage early?**
Not necessarily. It comes down to "opportunity cost." If your mortgage interest rate is very low (e.g., 3%), but you can safely earn 8% by investing your extra cash in an index fund, it makes more mathematical sense to invest the money rather than pay off the cheap debt. However, paying off a mortgage provides a guaranteed, risk-free return (equal to your interest rate) and immense psychological peace of mind.

**Will my monthly payment drop if I make extra payments?**
No. Unless you specifically request a "recast" from your lender, your required monthly payment will stay exactly the same. What changes is how much of that payment goes to interest vs. principal, and the date the loan reaches zero.
