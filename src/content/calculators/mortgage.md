## How Are Mortgage Payments Calculated?

The math behind a standard fixed-rate mortgage is based on an amortization formula. It calculates a fixed monthly payment that pays off both the principal (the actual amount you borrowed) and the interest (the cost of borrowing the money) over a set period (usually 15 or 30 years).

### The Amortization Formula

To calculate the monthly principal and interest payment manually, financial institutions use the following formula:

$$ M = P \frac{r(1+r)^n}{(1+r)^n - 1} $$

Where:
- **$M$** = Total monthly payment
- **$P$** = Principal loan amount (home price minus down payment)
- **$r$** = Monthly interest rate (annual interest rate divided by 12)
- **$n$** = Number of payments (loan term in years multiplied by 12)

**Example Calculation:**
Imagine you are buying a $300,000 home with a 20% down payment ($60,000). Your loan principal $P$ is $240,000. You secure a 30-year fixed loan at an annual interest rate of 5.0%.

- $P = 240,000$
- $r = 0.05 / 12 = 0.004167$
- $n = 30 \times 12 = 360$

$$ M = 240,000 \times \frac{0.004167(1+0.004167)^{360}}{(1+0.004167)^{360} - 1} $$
$$ M \approx \$1,288.37 $$

Your monthly principal and interest payment would be **$1,288.37**.

## Factors That Affect Your Monthly Payment

While principal and interest are the core of your loan, there are other costs factored into a true mortgage payment (PITI):

1. **Property Taxes:** Local governments charge an annual tax on your property. Lenders usually divide the annual amount by 12 and add it to your monthly payment.
2. **Homeowners Insurance:** Lenders require you to insure the property against fire, theft, and other disasters.
3. **Private Mortgage Insurance (PMI):** If your down payment is less than 20%, lenders usually require you to pay PMI to protect them in case of default.
4. **HOA Fees:** If you buy in a managed community, you may have Homeowners Association fees.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is a good interest rate for a mortgage?**
Interest rates fluctuate daily based on the broader economy, inflation, and the Federal Reserve. A "good" rate depends on the current market. Historically, rates under 5% are considered excellent, while the historical average hovers around 7% to 8%. Your personal credit score also heavily influences the rate you receive.

**Should I choose a 15-year or 30-year mortgage?**
A 30-year mortgage offers lower monthly payments, making the home more affordable month-to-month. A 15-year mortgage has higher monthly payments, but you will pay significantly less in total interest over the life of the loan and build equity much faster.

**How much house can I afford?**
A common rule of thumb is the 28/36 rule. This means your maximum household expenses should not exceed 28% of your gross monthly income, and your total debt (including the mortgage, car loans, and credit cards) should not exceed 36% of your gross income. Use our calculator to adjust the home price until the monthly payment fits comfortably within your 28% limit.

**Does this calculator work for refinancing?**
Yes. If you are considering refinancing, enter your current loan balance as the "Home Price" with a $0 down payment, and input the new interest rate and loan term. Compare the new monthly payment to your current one to see if refinancing makes financial sense.
