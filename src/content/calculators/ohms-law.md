## About the Ohm's Law Calculator

**Ohm's Law** is the foundational equation of electrical engineering and electronics. It defines the precise relationship between Voltage, Current, and Resistance in any electrical circuit. Whether you are an electrical engineering student, a DIY electronics hobbyist, or a professional electrician, this law is the starting point for every circuit calculation.

---

## The Formula

Ohm's Law states that voltage equals current multiplied by resistance:

$$ V = I \times R $$

Because of this, you can solve for any one of the three variables when you know the other two:

$$ I = \frac{V}{R} \qquad R = \frac{V}{I} $$

Where:
- **$V$** = Voltage, measured in Volts (V)
- **$I$** = Current, measured in Amperes (A)
- **$R$** = Resistance, measured in Ohms (Ω)

**Example:** A 12V car battery powers a car headlight with 4Ω of resistance. How much current flows?
$$ I = \frac{V}{R} = \frac{12}{4} = 3 \text{ Amperes} $$

---

## Electrical Power and Ohm's Law

Combining Ohm's Law with the power formula ($P = V \times I$) gives you a complete toolkit for any circuit:

$$ P = V \times I = I^2 \times R = \frac{V^2}{R} $$

If the headlight above draws 3A at 12V, its power rating is:
$$ P = 12 \times 3 = 36 \text{ Watts} $$

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does Ohm's Law apply to all materials?**
Ohm's Law applies to **ohmic** materials (like copper wire) where resistance stays constant regardless of voltage or current. **Non-ohmic** devices like diodes, transistors, and LEDs do not follow Ohm's Law because their resistance changes based on operating conditions.

**What happens when resistance is zero (a short circuit)?**
When $R = 0$, the formula $I = V/R$ approaches infinity — meaning an extremely large current flows. This is a short circuit. In practice, the wire's own small resistance limits the current, but it is high enough to generate dangerous heat and cause fires.

**What is the difference between AC and DC for Ohm's Law?**
For DC (direct current) circuits, Ohm's Law applies directly. For AC (alternating current) circuits, resistance becomes **impedance** — which includes the effects of capacitors and inductors — and the math becomes more complex using complex numbers.
