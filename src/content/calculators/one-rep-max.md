## About the One Rep Max Calculator

The **One Rep Max (1RM)** is the maximum amount of weight you can lift for a single repetition of an exercise with proper form. It is the gold standard measurement in strength training for gauging absolute strength and for programming workout intensities as percentages of 1RM.

> **Safety Note:** Actually attempting a true 1RM is risky for beginners. Use an estimation formula instead.

---

## The Epley Formula (Most Common)

$$ \text{1RM} = w \times \left(1 + \frac{r}{30}\right) $$

Where:
- **$w$** = Weight lifted
- **$r$** = Repetitions performed

**Example:** You bench press 80 kg for 8 reps:
$$ \text{1RM} = 80 \times \left(1 + \frac{8}{30}\right) = 80 \times 1.267 = 101.3 \text{ kg} $$

---

## Brzycki Formula (Slightly more accurate at lower reps)

$$ \text{1RM} = \frac{w \times 36}{37 - r} $$

For the same 80 kg × 8 reps:
$$ \text{1RM} = \frac{80 \times 36}{37 - 8} = \frac{2880}{29} = 99.3 \text{ kg} $$

Both formulas become less accurate above 12 reps.

## Training Percentages of 1RM

| Goal | % of 1RM | Reps |
|:---|:---|:---|
| Maximal Strength | 90–100% | 1–3 |
| Strength | 80–90% | 3–6 |
| Hypertrophy (Muscle Size) | 65–80% | 6–12 |
| Muscular Endurance | 50–65% | 12–20 |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How often should I test my 1RM?**
Strength programs typically test 1RM every 4–12 weeks at the end of a training cycle. Testing too frequently is counterproductive and increases injury risk.

**Does 1RM differ between exercises?**
Yes. A person's squat 1RM, bench press 1RM, and deadlift 1RM are all independent measurements. Elite powerlifters often specialize in maximizing all three.
