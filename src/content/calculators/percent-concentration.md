## About the Percent Concentration Calculator

In chemistry, **Percent Concentration** is a common way to express how much solute (the substance being dissolved) is present in a solution (the total mixture). Our calculator instantly handles the three main types of percent concentration: Mass/Mass, Volume/Volume, and Mass/Volume.

---

## The Three Formulas

### 1. Mass Percent (w/w%)
Used when dissolving a solid into a liquid, or mixing two solids (like metal alloys).
$$ \text{Mass \%} = \left( \frac{\text{Mass of Solute}}{\text{Total Mass of Solution}} \right) \times 100 $$
*Note: The Total Mass of Solution = Mass of Solute + Mass of Solvent.*
**Example:** Dissolving 10g of salt into 90g of water. 
Total mass = 100g. Mass % = $(10/100) \times 100 = \mathbf{10\%}$.

### 2. Volume Percent (v/v%)
Used when mixing two liquids together (like alcohol and water).
$$ \text{Volume \%} = \left( \frac{\text{Volume of Solute}}{\text{Total Volume of Solution}} \right) \times 100 $$
**Example:** A 750mL bottle of wine contains 90mL of pure ethanol. 
Volume % = $(90/750) \times 100 = \mathbf{12\% \text{ ABV (Alcohol By Volume)}}$.

### 3. Mass/Volume Percent (w/v%)
Often used in medicine (like IV saline bags) to express a solid dissolved in a liquid volume. It is specifically calculated as grams of solute per 100 mL of solution.
$$ \text{Mass/Volume \%} = \left( \frac{\text{Mass of Solute (g)}}{\text{Total Volume of Solution (mL)}} \right) \times 100 $$
**Example:** Normal medical saline is a 0.9% w/v solution. This means there are exactly 0.9 grams of NaCl (salt) in every 100 mL of water.

## Parts Per Million (PPM)

When the concentration is extremely low (such as measuring lead contaminants in drinking water), using percentages results in annoying decimals like 0.0001%. 

Instead, chemists use **Parts Per Million (ppm)**.
1% concentration = 10,000 ppm. 
To calculate ppm, you use the exact same formulas above, but multiply by 1,000,000 instead of 100.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why are there three different ways to calculate it?**
Different physical states of matter are easier to measure in different ways. It is very easy to weigh a solid powder on a scale (Mass). It is very easy to pour a liquid into a graduated cylinder (Volume). The three formulas accommodate whatever equipment you are using.

**Is 5% w/w the same as 5% v/v?**
No, because different substances have different densities. 5 grams of oil takes up a different amount of space than 5 grams of water. You must always specify which percentage type you are using.
