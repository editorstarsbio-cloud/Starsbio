## About the Percentage Change Calculator

**Percentage change** measures how much a value has increased or decreased relative to its original value. It is one of the most frequently used calculations in business reporting, financial analysis, economics, and everyday life.

---

## The Formula

$$ \text{Percentage Change} = \frac{\text{New Value} - \text{Old Value}}{\text{Old Value}} \times 100 $$

- **Positive result** = an increase
- **Negative result** = a decrease

**Example 1 — Price increase:** A product cost $80 last month and now costs $92.
$$ \% \text{ Change} = \frac{92 - 80}{80} \times 100 = \frac{12}{80} \times 100 = +15\% $$

**Example 2 — Stock decline:** A stock was $150 and fell to $127.50.
$$ \% \text{ Change} = \frac{127.50 - 150}{150} \times 100 = \frac{-22.50}{150} \times 100 = -15\% $$

---

## Percentage Change vs. Percentage Difference

These are NOT the same:
- **Percentage Change** compares a new value to an original/reference value (has direction: up or down).
- **Percentage Difference** compares any two values without designating which is the reference:

$$ \text{Percentage Difference} = \frac{|V_1 - V_2|}{\frac{V_1 + V_2}{2}} \times 100 $$

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why does a 50% drop require a 100% gain to recover?**
If a $100 stock drops 50%, it falls to $50. To get back to $100, it must gain $50 on a $50 base — that is a 100% gain. Losses and gains are asymmetric because the base changes.

**What is the difference between percentage change and percentage points?**
If an interest rate rises from 4% to 6%, it increased by **2 percentage points** — but the percentage change is $\frac{6-4}{4} \times 100 = 50\%$. These are completely different values. Percentage points measure absolute differences between percentages.
