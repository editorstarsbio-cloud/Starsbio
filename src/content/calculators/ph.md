## About the pH Calculator

**pH** measures the acidity or alkalinity of a solution on a scale from 0 to 14. It is one of the most critical measurements in chemistry, biology, environmental science, food production, and medicine. 

- pH < 7: **Acidic** (the lower the number, the stronger the acid)
- pH = 7: **Neutral** (pure water at 25°C)
- pH > 7: **Basic (Alkaline)** (the higher, the stronger the base)

---

## The pH Formula

pH is defined as the negative base-10 logarithm of the hydrogen ion concentration:

$$ \text{pH} = -\log_{10}[\text{H}^+] $$

Where $[\text{H}^+]$ is the concentration of hydrogen ions in moles per liter (mol/L).

**Example 1 — Find pH:** A solution has $[\text{H}^+] = 1 \times 10^{-3}$ mol/L:
$$ \text{pH} = -\log_{10}(10^{-3}) = -(-3) = 3 $$
This is a moderately acidic solution (similar to vinegar).

**Finding concentration from pH:**
$$ [\text{H}^+] = 10^{-\text{pH}} $$

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is the pH scale logarithmic?**
Because hydrogen ion concentrations span an enormous range (from $10^{-1}$ to $10^{-14}$ mol/L), a logarithmic scale compresses this into a manageable 0–14 range. Each one-unit change in pH represents a **10× change** in acidity.

**What is pOH?**
pOH measures hydroxide ion concentration: $\text{pOH} = -\log_{10}[\text{OH}^-]$. In water at 25°C, $\text{pH} + \text{pOH} = 14$ always.
