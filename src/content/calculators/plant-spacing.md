## About the Plant Spacing Calculator

Whether you are planting a small backyard vegetable garden, setting up an orchard, or planning a massive commercial agricultural field, proper spacing is critical. Our **Plant Spacing Calculator** tells you exactly how many plants or seeds you need to fill a specific area.

---

## Why Spacing Matters

Planting too close together forces plants to compete for sunlight, water, and soil nutrients, leading to stunted growth, poor yields, and increased susceptibility to fungal diseases (due to lack of airflow). Planting too far apart wastes valuable land and allows weeds to take over empty soil.

---

## The Formula

To calculate the total number of plants required, you divide the total usable area by the area required for each individual plant.

$$ \text{Total Plants} = \frac{\text{Total Area}}{\text{Row Spacing} \times \text{Plant Spacing}} $$

*Note: All measurements must be in the same unit (e.g., convert everything to square feet or square meters before dividing).*

**Example (Traditional Rows):**
You have a garden bed that is 10 feet wide and 20 feet long (Total Area = 200 sq ft). 
You want to plant tomatoes. The seed packet recommends 3 feet between rows, and 2 feet between plants within the row.
- Space per plant = $3 \text{ ft} \times 2 \text{ ft} = 6 \text{ sq ft}$.
- Total Plants = $200 / 6 = \mathbf{33.3 \text{ plants}}$. (You can fit 33 tomato plants).

## Planting Layouts

### 1. Square / Rectangular Layout
The standard farm and garden layout. Plants are placed in straight rows. It is easy to navigate, weed, and harvest, but it does not maximize space perfectly.

### 2. Triangular (Hexagonal) Layout
Often used in orchards. Plants in the second row are offset to sit in the "gaps" of the first row. This allows you to fit up to **15% more plants** in the same area while maintaining the exact same distance between every plant.
$$ \text{Plants (Triangular)} = \text{Plants (Square)} \times 1.154 $$

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is Square Foot Gardening?**
Square Foot Gardening is a high-density method for raised beds. You divide the bed into 1-foot by 1-foot squares. Instead of rows, you plant a specific number of seeds per square based on the plant's mature size (e.g., 1 tomato per square, 4 heads of lettuce per square, or 16 carrots per square).

**Do I need to leave room for paths?**
Yes! A common mistake for beginners is filling a 10x10 plot entirely with plants and then having no way to walk in to harvest the vegetables in the middle without crushing them. If your garden is wider than 4 feet, you must subtract the area needed for walking paths from your "Total Area" before calculating.
