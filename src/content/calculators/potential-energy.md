## About the Potential Energy Calculator

**Potential energy** is stored energy — energy an object possesses due to its position or configuration rather than its motion. The most common type is gravitational potential energy, which depends on an object's height above a reference point. When an object falls, its potential energy converts to kinetic energy.

---

## Gravitational Potential Energy

$$ PE = m \times g \times h $$

Where:
- **$PE$** = Potential Energy (Joules, J)
- **$m$** = Mass (kg)
- **$g$** = Acceleration due to gravity = 9.81 m/s²
- **$h$** = Height above reference point (m)

**Example:** A 5 kg book sitting on a shelf 2 meters above the floor:
$$ PE = 5 \times 9.81 \times 2 = 98.1 \text{ J} $$

When the book falls off the shelf, those 98.1 J convert entirely to kinetic energy just before impact.

---

## Elastic Potential Energy (Springs)

Elastic potential energy is stored in compressed or stretched springs:
$$ PE_{elastic} = \frac{1}{2} k x^2 $$
Where $k$ is the spring constant (N/m) and $x$ is the displacement from equilibrium.

---

## Energy Conservation

In a frictionless system, total mechanical energy is conserved:
$$ PE + KE = \text{constant} $$

A roller coaster's car has maximum PE at the top of a hill and maximum KE (and speed) at the bottom.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why is PE always defined relative to a reference point?**
Potential energy has no absolute value — only differences in PE matter physically. Choosing a reference (usually the ground or lowest point) gives us a convenient calculation baseline. The choice of reference doesn't affect the energy calculations.

**What is chemical potential energy?**
Food, fuel, and batteries store energy in chemical bonds. When those bonds break (combustion, digestion, discharge), the energy is released as heat, light, or electrical energy. This is also a form of potential energy at the molecular level.
