## About the Power Calculator (Physics)

In physics, **power** measures the rate at which work is done or energy is transferred. It answers the question: "How quickly is energy being used or produced?" Power is central to mechanical engineering, electrical circuits, thermodynamics, and everyday devices.

---

## The Formulas

$$ P = \frac{W}{t} \qquad P = F \times v \qquad P = \frac{E}{t} $$

Where:
- **$P$** = Power (Watts, W)
- **$W$** = Work done (Joules, J)
- **$t$** = Time (seconds, s)
- **$F$** = Force (Newtons, N)
- **$v$** = Velocity (m/s)
- **$E$** = Energy transferred (J)

**Example:** A motor lifts a 200 kg load by 5 meters in 10 seconds:
- Work = $200 \times 9.81 \times 5 = 9,810$ J
$$ P = \frac{9,810}{10} = 981 \text{ W} \approx 1 \text{ kW} $$

---

## Electrical Power

In electrical circuits, power is:
$$ P = V \times I = I^2 R = \frac{V^2}{R} $$

A 120V household circuit drawing 10A uses: $P = 120 \times 10 = 1,200$ W = **1.2 kW**.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the difference between power and energy?**
Energy is the total amount of work done (Joules or kWh). Power is the rate of energy use (Watts = Joules per second). Your electricity bill charges you for energy (kWh), not power. A 1,000W microwave running for 1 hour uses 1 kWh of energy.

**Why is horsepower used for cars?**
James Watt coined "horsepower" in the late 1700s to help sell steam engines by comparing them to horses. One mechanical horsepower = 550 foot-pounds per second = 745.7 Watts. Car engines are still rated in hp due to historical tradition.
