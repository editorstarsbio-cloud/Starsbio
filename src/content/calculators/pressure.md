## About the Pressure Calculator

**Pressure** is the force applied perpendicular to the surface of an object per unit area. It appears in fluid mechanics, meteorology, engineering, medicine (blood pressure), and everyday life (tire pressure, cooking with a pressure cooker).

---

## The Formula

$$ P = \frac{F}{A} $$

Where:
- **$P$** = Pressure (Pascals, Pa)
- **$F$** = Force applied (Newtons, N)
- **$A$** = Area over which the force is distributed (m²)

**Example:** A 700 N force applied over 0.05 m²:
$$ P = \frac{700}{0.05} = 14,000 \text{ Pa} = 14 \text{ kPa} $$

## Types of Pressure

- **Absolute Pressure:** Pressure measured relative to a perfect vacuum (zero pressure). All thermodynamic calculations use absolute pressure.
- **Gauge Pressure:** Pressure measured relative to atmospheric pressure. Tire pressure gauges show gauge pressure ($P_{gauge} = P_{abs} - P_{atm}$).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Why do sharp objects penetrate better than blunt ones?**
Pressure = Force ÷ Area. A sharp knife concentrates the same cutting force over an extremely tiny area, creating very high pressure at the blade edge. A blunt knife spreads the same force over a much larger area, producing far less pressure.

**What is blood pressure?**
Blood pressure is measured in mmHg (millimeters of mercury). A reading of "120/80" means 120 mmHg systolic (peak pressure during heartbeat) over 80 mmHg diastolic (pressure between beats). Normal range is below 120/80.
