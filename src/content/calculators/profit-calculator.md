## Gross Profit vs. Net Profit

It is vital to understand the difference between gross and net profit:

### 1. Gross Profit
Gross profit only subtracts the direct costs of producing your goods (COGS - Cost of Goods Sold).
$$ \text{Gross Profit} = \text{Revenue} - \text{COGS} $$

### 2. Net Profit
Net profit subtracts ALL expenses, including operating expenses (rent, marketing, payroll), interest, and taxes. This is the true "bottom line."
$$ \text{Net Profit} = \text{Gross Profit} - \text{Operating Expenses} - \text{Taxes} $$

**Example:** You run an e-commerce store.
- You sell 1,000 shirts at $25 each (Revenue = $25,000)
- The shirts cost you $10 each to buy and print (COGS = $10,000)
- **Gross Profit = $15,000**
- You pay $2,000 for Shopify/hosting, $3,000 for Facebook ads, and $1,000 for shipping (Operating Expenses = $6,000)
- **Net Profit = $15,000 - $6,000 = $9,000**

## Profit Margins

Profit is an absolute dollar amount. Profit *margin* is a percentage, which is better for comparing efficiency.

$$ \text{Gross Profit Margin} = \left(\frac{\text{Gross Profit}}{\text{Revenue}}\right) \times 100 $$

$$ \text{Net Profit Margin} = \left(\frac{\text{Net Profit}}{\text{Revenue}}\right) \times 100 $$

In the example above, the Net Profit Margin is $(9,000 / 25,000) \times 100 = \mathbf{36\%}$.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is a "good" profit margin?**
It varies wildly by industry. Grocery stores operate on razor-thin net margins (1-3%) but make it up in massive volume. Software (SaaS) companies often have net margins of 20-30%. As a general rule across all industries, a 10% net profit margin is considered average, 20% is high, and 5% is low.

**What is the difference between markup and profit margin?**
Markup is based on cost; margin is based on revenue. If you buy a product for $50 and sell it for $100, your markup is 100% (you doubled the cost). But your profit margin is 50% ($50 profit / $100 revenue).
