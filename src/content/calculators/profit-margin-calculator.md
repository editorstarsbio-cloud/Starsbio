## How is Profit Margin Calculated?

Profit margin is always expressed as a percentage. A 20% profit margin means that for every $100 in revenue the business generates, it keeps $20 in pure profit, while the remaining $80 was spent on costs.

### The Profit Margin Formula

To calculate the profit margin, you must first find your Net Profit by subtracting your total costs from your total revenue. Then, divide the profit by the revenue and multiply by 100.

$$ \text{Net Profit} = \text{Total Revenue} - \text{Total Costs} $$

$$ \text{Profit Margin} = \left( \frac{\text{Net Profit}}{\text{Total Revenue}} \right) \times 100 $$

**Example Calculation:**
Imagine you run a retail business. Last month, you generated $50,000 in total sales revenue. However, after paying for inventory, employee salaries, rent, and software subscriptions, your total costs were $40,000.

- Revenue = $50,000
- Costs = $40,000

First, calculate the Net Profit:
$$ \text{Net Profit} = 50,000 - 40,000 = \$10,000 $$

Next, calculate the Margin:
$$ \text{Margin} = \left( \frac{10000}{50000} \right) \times 100 $$
$$ \text{Margin} = 0.20 \times 100 = 20\% $$

Your profit margin is **20%**.

## Gross Margin vs. Net Margin

In professional finance, accountants track several different types of profit margins to identify where a business might be losing money.

1. **Gross Profit Margin:** This only deducts the Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) from revenue. It tells you how efficiently you are producing your actual product, before paying for overhead like rent, marketing, and administration.
2. **Operating Profit Margin:** This deducts COGS *and* operating expenses (like rent and salaries), but it ignores taxes and interest payments on debt.
3. **Net Profit Margin:** The absolute bottom line. This deducts *all* expenses, including taxes and interest. This is the true metric of business profitability.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the difference between Margin and Markup?**
This is the most common mistake made by new business owners. **Margin** is profit expressed as a percentage of *Revenue*. **Markup** is profit expressed as a percentage of *Cost*. 

If you buy a shirt for $50 and sell it for $100, your profit is $50.
- Your Markup is 100% (because $50 is 100% of your $50 cost).
- Your Margin is 50% (because $50 is 50% of your $100 revenue).
Margin can never exceed 100%, but Markup can be infinitely high.

**What is a "good" profit margin?**
A "good" margin depends heavily on the industry. Supermarkets and grocery stores often operate on extremely thin net margins (1% to 3%) but survive through massive sales volume. Software (SaaS) companies often have incredibly high net margins (20% to 40%) because it costs almost nothing to duplicate digital software. Across all industries, a 10% net profit margin is generally considered an average, healthy benchmark.
