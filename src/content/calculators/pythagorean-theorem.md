## About the Pythagorean Theorem Calculator

The **Pythagorean Theorem** is one of the most celebrated equations in all of mathematics. It defines the exact relationship between the three sides of any right-angled triangle, and it has been used for over 2,500 years in architecture, navigation, carpentry, and engineering.

---

## The Formula

For a right triangle with legs $a$ and $b$ and hypotenuse $c$ (the longest side, opposite the right angle):

$$ a^2 + b^2 = c^2 $$

Solving for the hypotenuse: $c = \sqrt{a^2 + b^2}$

Solving for a missing leg: $a = \sqrt{c^2 - b^2}$

**Example:** A right triangle has legs of 3 and 4. Find the hypotenuse:
$$ c = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} = 5 $$
This is the famous **3-4-5 Pythagorean triple**.

---

## Common Pythagorean Triples

These are sets of whole numbers that perfectly satisfy $a^2 + b^2 = c^2$:

| a | b | c |
|:---|:---|:---|
| 3 | 4 | 5 |
| 5 | 12 | 13 |
| 8 | 15 | 17 |
| 7 | 24 | 25 |

Carpenters use the **3-4-5 rule** constantly to check that corners are perfectly square.

## Real-World Applications

1. **Construction:** Checking that a foundation or deck corner forms a true 90° angle.
2. **Navigation:** Calculating the straight-line ("as the crow flies") distance between two GPS coordinates.
3. **Computer Graphics:** Calculating the distance between any two pixels on a screen.
4. **Baseball:** The distance from home plate to second base is calculated using the Pythagorean theorem applied to the 90-foot basepaths.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does it work for any triangle?**
No. The Pythagorean theorem only applies to **right triangles** (triangles with one 90° angle). For other triangles, you need the Law of Cosines: $c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab\cos(C)$.

**How do I check if a triangle is a right triangle?**
If the squares of the two shorter sides add up to exactly the square of the longest side ($a^2 + b^2 = c^2$), the triangle has a right angle.
