## About the Quadratic Equation Calculator

The **Quadratic Equation Calculator** solves any equation in the form $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ using the famous **Quadratic Formula**. Quadratic equations appear in physics (projectile motion), engineering (structural loads), economics (profit maximization), and virtually every STEM discipline.

---

## The Quadratic Formula

$$ x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a} $$

This formula always gives the solutions (roots) of the quadratic equation, as long as $a \neq 0$.

**Example:** Solve $2x^2 - 4x - 6 = 0$:
- $a = 2$, $b = -4$, $c = -6$

$$ x = \frac{-(-4) \pm \sqrt{(-4)^2 - 4(2)(-6)}}{2(2)} $$
$$ x = \frac{4 \pm \sqrt{16 + 48}}{4} = \frac{4 \pm \sqrt{64}}{4} = \frac{4 \pm 8}{4} $$

Two solutions: $x = \frac{4+8}{4} = 3$ and $x = \frac{4-8}{4} = -1$

## The Discriminant: Predicting Solution Types

The expression under the square root, $b^2 - 4ac$, is called the **discriminant** ($\Delta$). Its value tells you how many real solutions exist before you do any calculation:

| Discriminant | Solutions |
|:---|:---|
| $\Delta > 0$ | Two distinct real roots |
| $\Delta = 0$ | One repeated real root |
| $\Delta < 0$ | No real roots (two complex roots) |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is a "root" of an equation?**
A root (or solution) is any value of $x$ that makes the equation equal to zero. Geometrically, the roots of a quadratic are the x-coordinates where the parabola ($y = ax^2 + bx + c$) crosses the x-axis.

**What if the discriminant is negative?**
When $\Delta < 0$, the square root contains a negative number, which has no real value. The solutions are **complex numbers** involving $i$ (the imaginary unit, where $i = \sqrt{-1}$). This means the parabola never crosses the x-axis.

**Can I solve a quadratic by factoring instead?**
Yes, if the equation factors neatly. For example, $x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0$ factors as $(x-2)(x-3) = 0$, giving $x=2$ or $x=3$ immediately. The quadratic formula is the fallback that always works when factoring is difficult or impossible.
