## About the Recipe Scaling Calculator

Our **Recipe Scaling Calculator** adjusts any recipe to produce more or fewer servings. Whether you're cooking for a dinner party of 20 or scaling a restaurant recipe down for a family meal, this tool recalculates every ingredient proportionally in seconds.

---

## How Recipe Scaling Works

Scaling is simple proportional math:

$$ \text{New Amount} = \text{Original Amount} \times \frac{\text{Desired Servings}}{\text{Original Servings}} $$

**Example:** A cookie recipe serves 24 and calls for 2 cups of flour. You want to make 36 cookies:
$$ \text{New Flour} = 2 \times \frac{36}{24} = 2 \times 1.5 = 3 \text{ cups} $$

---

## Important Exceptions: What Doesn't Scale Linearly

Not every ingredient scales perfectly. These require judgment:

- **Salt and strong spices:** Start at 75% of the calculated amount; taste and adjust. Flavors intensify non-linearly in large batches.
- **Leavening agents (baking powder, baking soda):** Scale accurately for small changes. For very large batches (4×+), slightly reduce the proportion.
- **Cooking time:** Scaling a recipe does NOT proportionally change cooking time. A doubled batch of meatballs takes roughly the same baking time per meatball — but a doubled cake may need more time.
- **Pan size:** Doubling a recipe requires a larger pan, which affects heat distribution and cooking time.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I scale a recipe by any factor?**
You can scale by any factor mathematically, but extreme scaling (8× or more) may affect the final product quality, especially for baked goods where chemistry matters.

**What does "Baker's Percentage" mean?**
In professional baking, each ingredient is expressed as a percentage of the total flour weight. Flour is always 100%. This system makes scaling any bread or pastry recipe trivially easy.
