## About the Rental Yield Calculator

**Rental yield** is the annual return you earn from a property's rent, expressed as a percentage of the property's value. It is the first metric every property investor checks to determine whether a buy-to-let investment is financially viable.

---

## Gross vs. Net Rental Yield

### Gross Rental Yield (Quick estimate)
$$ \text{Gross Yield} = \frac{\text{Annual Rent}}{\text{Property Value}} \times 100 $$

**Example:** Property worth $300,000 renting for $1,800/month ($21,600/year):
$$ \text{Gross Yield} = \frac{21,600}{300,000} \times 100 = 7.2\% $$

### Net Rental Yield (More accurate)
Deduct annual costs (property taxes, insurance, maintenance, management fees, vacancies) from rent first:

$$ \text{Net Yield} = \frac{\text{Annual Rent} - \text{Annual Costs}}{\text{Property Value}} \times 100 $$

If annual costs are $5,000: Net Yield = $\frac{16,600}{300,000} \times 100 = 5.53\%$

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is a higher rental yield always better?**
Not necessarily. High yields often come with higher risk — less desirable locations, older properties requiring more maintenance, or higher vacancy rates. Low-yield properties in prime locations often deliver superior capital appreciation (property value growth).

**How does rental yield differ from cap rate?**
They measure similar things but have different precise definitions in different markets. In the UK, "rental yield" is commonly used; in the US, "cap rate" is standard. Cap rate always uses Net Operating Income; rental yield can be gross or net depending on context.
