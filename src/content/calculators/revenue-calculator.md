## About the Revenue Calculator

**Revenue** (often called the "top line") is the total amount of money brought in by a company's operations, before any expenses, taxes, or costs are deducted. Our **Revenue Calculator** helps businesses project their total sales income based on units sold and average price.

---

## The Formula

The most basic revenue equation is:

$$ \text{Total Revenue} = \text{Price per Unit} \times \text{Number of Units Sold} $$

**Example:** A software company sells 500 licenses of its software at $199 each.
$$ \text{Total Revenue} = 500 \times 199 = \$99,500 $$

### Service-Based Revenue
If you run a service business (like a consulting firm or agency):
$$ \text{Total Revenue} = \text{Billable Hours} \times \text{Hourly Rate} $$

**Example:** A consultant works 120 billable hours in a month at $150/hour.
$$ \text{Total Revenue} = 120 \times 150 = \$18,000 $$

---

## Revenue vs. Profit

It is critical to distinguish between revenue and profit:
- **Revenue:** The total money brought in. (Top Line)
- **Profit:** The money left over after paying all expenses. (Bottom Line)

You can have millions in revenue and still go bankrupt if your expenses exceed your revenue.

## Types of Revenue

| Type | Description |
|:---|:---|
| **Operating Revenue** | Income from a company's core business activities (e.g., Apple selling iPhones). |
| **Non-Operating Revenue** | Income from secondary sources (e.g., interest earned, dividend income, lawsuit settlements). |
| **Recurring Revenue** | Predictable, ongoing income (e.g., Netflix monthly subscriptions). Highly valued by investors. |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is MRR and ARR?**
MRR (Monthly Recurring Revenue) and ARR (Annual Recurring Revenue) are metrics used primarily by SaaS (Software as a Service) businesses to measure predictable subscription income. A business with 1,000 active subscribers paying $50/month has an MRR of $50,000 and an ARR of $600,000.

**How does discounting affect revenue?**
Discounting lowers the price per unit. To maintain the same revenue, you must proportionally increase the volume of units sold. If you drop prices by 20%, you must sell 25% more units just to break even on total revenue.
