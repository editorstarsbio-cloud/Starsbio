## About the Return on Investment (ROI) Calculator

**Return on Investment (ROI)** is the ultimate metric for evaluating the profitability of any expenditure. Whether you are buying stocks, launching a marketing campaign, or purchasing real estate, our **ROI Calculator** tells you exactly how much bang you're getting for your buck.

---

## The Formula

$$ \text{ROI} = \left( \frac{\text{Net Return}}{\text{Cost of Investment}} \right) \times 100 $$

Where:
$$ \text{Net Return} = \text{Final Value (or Total Revenue)} - \text{Cost of Investment} $$

**Example (Stock Market):**
You buy 100 shares of a company at $50/share (Total Cost = $5,000). A year later, you sell them at $65/share (Total Value = $6,500).
- Net Return = $6,500 - $5,000 = $1,500
- ROI = $(1,500 / 5,000) \times 100 = \mathbf{30\%}$

**Example (Marketing Campaign):**
You spend $2,000 on Google Ads. Those ads generate $5,500 in new sales.
- Net Return = $5,500 - $2,000 = $3,500
- ROI = $(3,500 / 2,000) \times 100 = \mathbf{175\%}$

## Annualized ROI

Standard ROI doesn't account for time. A 50% ROI over 1 year is incredible; a 50% ROI over 20 years is terrible. **Annualized ROI** normalizes the return to a yearly rate:

$$ \text{Annualized ROI} = \left( (1 + \text{Total ROI})^{\frac{1}{n}} - 1 \right) \times 100 $$
*(Where $n$ is the number of years)*

If an investment grows 50% over 5 years:
$$ \text{Annualized ROI} = \left( (1.50)^{\frac{1}{5}} - 1 \right) = \left( 1.50^{0.2} - 1 \right) = 1.084 - 1 = \mathbf{8.4\% \text{ per year}} $$

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is a "good" ROI?**
The S&P 500 historically returns about 7-10% annually (before inflation). For passive investments, anything consistently beating 10% is excellent. For active business investments (like marketing or new equipment), companies often look for ROIs of 50-100% or more to justify the risk.

**What is the difference between ROI and Profit Margin?**
Profit margin measures how efficiently a company turns revenue into profit over a period (e.g., a quarter). ROI measures the return of a *specific investment* relative to its specific cost.
