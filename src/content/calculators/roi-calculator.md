## About the ROI Calculator (Return on Investment)

The **ROI Calculator** is one of the most critical tools in business, real estate, and investing. Return on Investment (ROI) is a universal financial metric used to evaluate the profitability and efficiency of an investment. 

Whether you are buying stocks, launching a marketing campaign, purchasing rental property, or upgrading factory equipment, calculating your ROI tells you exactly how much profit you made for every dollar you spent.

---

## How to Calculate ROI

ROI is always expressed as a percentage. This makes it incredibly easy to compare completely different investments side-by-side (for example, comparing the return on a real estate flip versus a stock market index fund).

### The Standard ROI Formula

The mathematical formula for Return on Investment is:

$$ \text{ROI} = \left( \frac{\text{Net Profit}}{\text{Total Cost of Investment}} \right) \times 100 $$

Or, written differently:

$$ \text{ROI} = \left( \frac{\text{Final Value} - \text{Initial Cost}}{\text{Initial Cost}} \right) \times 100 $$

**Example Calculation:**
Imagine you buy 100 shares of a company's stock for $10 each. Your Total Investment Cost is $1,000. One year later, you sell all the shares for $15 each, giving you a Final Value of $1,500.

- Net Profit = $1,500 - $1,000 = $500
- Total Cost = $1,000

$$ \text{ROI} = \left( \frac{500}{1000} \right) \times 100 $$
$$ \text{ROI} = 0.50 \times 100 = 50\% $$

Your Return on Investment is **50%**.

---

## Annualized ROI

One of the biggest limitations of the standard ROI formula is that it does not account for *time*. 

Making a 50% ROI in one month is incredible. Making a 50% ROI over 20 years is terrible. To solve this, investors use **Annualized ROI**, which calculates what your average yearly return was over the holding period.

### Annualized ROI Formula

$$ \text{Annualized ROI} = \left( \left(1 + \frac{\text{ROI}}{100}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}} - 1 \right) \times 100 $$
*(Where $n$ is the number of years the investment was held).*

Using our previous example of a 50% total ROI, if it took you 5 years to achieve it:
$$ \text{Annualized ROI} = \left( (1 + 0.50)^{\frac{1}{5}} - 1 \right) \times 100 \approx 8.45\% $$
Your true annual growth rate was 8.45% per year.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is a "good" ROI?**
A "good" ROI depends entirely on the asset class and your risk tolerance. Historically, the S&P 500 stock index has returned an annualized ROI of about 7% to 10%. Anything above 10% in passive investing is generally considered excellent. For active businesses and startups, target ROIs are often much higher (15% to 30%+) to compensate for the extreme risk of failure.

**Can ROI be negative?**
Yes. If you lose money on an investment, your net profit is negative, resulting in a negative ROI. For example, if you buy a car for $10,000 and sell it for $8,000, your ROI is -20%.

**Does ROI factor in taxes and fees?**
The basic ROI formula does not. To get your *True* ROI, you must subtract all trading fees, property taxes, maintenance costs, and capital gains taxes from your "Final Value" before calculating the net profit.
