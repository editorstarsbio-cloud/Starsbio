## About the Rule of 72 Calculator

The **Rule of 72** is one of the most famous and useful mental math shortcuts in finance. It allows you to quickly estimate how many years it will take for an investment to double in value at a fixed annual rate of return. Our **Rule of 72 Calculator** applies this principle instantly.

---

## The Formula

The formula is elegantly simple:

$$ \text{Years to Double} = \frac{72}{\text{Interest Rate}} $$

*(Note: Use the whole number for the interest rate, not the decimal. For 8%, use 8, not 0.08).*

**Examples:**
- If you invest in the S&P 500 and expect a **10%** annual return: $72 / 10 =$ **7.2 years** to double your money.
- If you put money in a high-yield savings account earning **4%**: $72 / 4 =$ **18 years** to double.
- If you leave money in a standard checking account earning **0.1%**: $72 / 0.1 =$ **720 years** to double.

---

## Why the Rule of 72 Works

The Rule of 72 is an approximation of the complex logarithmic formula for compound interest: $t = \ln(2) / \ln(1 + r)$. 

The natural log of 2 is approximately 0.693. Therefore, a "Rule of 69.3" would technically be more mathematically accurate. However, 69.3 is terrible for mental math because it doesn't divide easily. The number 72 is highly divisible (by 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 9, and 12), making it perfect for quick estimates in your head. 

The Rule of 72 is highly accurate for interest rates between 6% and 10%. For extremely high rates (like 25%), it starts to lose accuracy.

---

## Using it for Inflation

The Rule of 72 works in reverse, too. You can use it to determine how long it will take for inflation to cut the purchasing power of your money in half.

If average inflation is **3%** per year, it will take $72 / 3 =$ **24 years** for the cost of living to double. This means if you retire at 65 and live to 89, you will need exactly twice as much money per month to buy the same groceries at the end of your retirement as you did at the beginning.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is there a Rule of 114 or 144?**
Yes! The Rule of 114 tells you how long it takes to **triple** your money ($114 / \text{Rate}$). The Rule of 144 tells you how long it takes to **quadruple** your money ($144 / \text{Rate}$).

**Does the Rule of 72 assume compounding?**
Yes, it exclusively assumes compound interest. If you are earning simple interest (which does not compound), the Rule of 72 will be completely inaccurate. (To double money with simple interest, you just divide 100 by the interest rate).
