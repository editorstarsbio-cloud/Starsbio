## About the Sales Growth Calculator

The **Sales Growth Calculator** determines the rate at which a company's revenue is increasing or decreasing over a specific period. It is a critical metric for business owners, investors, and analysts evaluating the health and momentum of a company.

---

## The Formula

$$ \text{Sales Growth Rate} = \left( \frac{\text{Current Period Sales} - \text{Prior Period Sales}}{\text{Prior Period Sales}} \right) \times 100 $$

**Example (Year-over-Year):**
- 2023 Sales: $500,000
- 2024 Sales: $620,000

$$ \text{Growth Rate} = \left( \frac{620,000 - 500,000}{500,000} \right) \times 100 = \left( \frac{120,000}{500,000} \right) \times 100 = \mathbf{24\%} $$

---

## Common Timeframes for Sales Growth

1. **Year-over-Year (YoY):** Compares a period (like Q1) to the exact same period in the previous year. This is the gold standard because it eliminates seasonal fluctuations (e.g., retail spikes in Q4).
2. **Quarter-over-Quarter (QoQ):** Compares the current quarter to the immediately preceding quarter. Useful for fast-growing startups, but vulnerable to seasonality.
3. **Month-over-Month (MoM):** Compares current month to the previous month. Used for highly granular tracking of new marketing campaigns.

## Calculating Average Annual Growth Rate (AAGR vs CAGR)

If you have sales data spanning multiple years, calculating the average growth rate is important.

**Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR)** is the most accurate method because it accounts for compounding:
$$ \text{CAGR} = \left( \frac{\text{Ending Value}}{\text{Beginning Value}} \right)^{\frac{1}{n}} - 1 $$

**Example:** Sales grew from $100k in 2020 to $250k in 2024 (4 years):
$$ \text{CAGR} = \left( \frac{250}{100} \right)^{\frac{1}{4}} - 1 = (2.5)^{0.25} - 1 = 1.257 - 1 = \mathbf{25.7\% \text{ per year}} $$

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is a healthy sales growth rate?**
It depends on the company's maturity. A mature, established company (like Coca-Cola) might target 3-5% YoY growth. A funded tech startup might be expected to grow at 100-200% YoY in its early stages. For a typical small business, 10-15% sustained annual growth is very healthy.

**Can sales growth be a bad thing?**
Yes, if it outpaces your operational capacity. Rapid growth can lead to cash flow crises (needing to buy inventory before customers pay), degraded customer service, and employee burnout. This is known as "overtrading."
