## The Formula

The growth of a savings account with regular monthly contributions uses the Future Value of an Annuity formula combined with the compound interest on the initial principal:

$$ \text{Total} = P(1 + \frac{r}{n})^{nt} + \text{PMT} \times \frac{(1 + \frac{r}{n})^{nt} - 1}{\frac{r}{n}} $$

Where:
- **$P$** = Initial Deposit
- **$r$** = Annual Interest Rate
- **$n$** = Compounding periods per year (usually 12 for monthly)
- **$t$** = Number of years
- **PMT** = Monthly Contribution

**Example:**
You start with $1,000 in a High-Yield Savings Account (HYSA) paying 4%. You commit to depositing $200 every month for 5 years.
- The initial $1,000 grows to $1,221.
- The $200 monthly deposits grow to $13,260.
- **Total Savings = $14,481** (You contributed $13,000; the bank paid you $1,481 in interest).

## Emergency Funds: The First Goal

Before investing in the stock market or saving for a house, personal finance experts unanimously agree that your first goal must be an Emergency Fund.

An emergency fund is 3 to 6 months' worth of living expenses kept in a highly liquid, accessible savings account. If your rent, food, and utilities cost $3,000 a month, your emergency fund should be between $9,000 and $18,000. This protects you from financial ruin in the event of a job loss, medical emergency, or major car repair.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Where is the best place to keep savings?**
For short-term goals (under 3-5 years) or emergency funds, the money should be in a High-Yield Savings Account (HYSA) or a Certificate of Deposit (CD). Do not put money you need in the short term into the stock market; a market crash could wipe out 30% of your savings the week before you need to buy your house.

**Is compound interest really that powerful?**
Yes, but mostly over long periods. In the first few years, your balance grows linearly based on your deposits. But after 10 or 20 years, the exponential curve kicks in, and the interest you earn begins to dwarf your actual contributions. This is why starting to save at age 25 is exponentially easier than starting at age 40.
