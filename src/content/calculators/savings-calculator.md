## The Formula for Savings Growth

If you have both an initial deposit and regular contributions, the total future value is the sum of two parts:

**Part 1 — Growth of initial deposit:**
$$ FV_1 = PV \times (1 + r)^n $$

**Part 2 — Growth of regular contributions:**
$$ FV_2 = PMT \times \frac{(1 + r)^n - 1}{r} $$

**Total:** $FV = FV_1 + FV_2$

**Example:** $5,000 initial deposit, $300/month contribution, 4% annual interest (0.333%/month), for 5 years (60 months):
- $FV_1 = 5000 \times (1.00333)^{60} = 5000 \times 1.2208 = \$6,104$
- $FV_2 = 300 \times \frac{(1.00333)^{60} - 1}{0.00333} = 300 \times 66.30 = \$19,889$
- **Total = $25,993**

## Savings Goal Planning

To find the required monthly contribution to hit a target savings goal:
$$ PMT = \frac{FV - PV(1+r)^n}{\frac{(1+r)^n - 1}{r}} $$

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What interest rate should I use?**
For a high-yield savings account (HYSA), use current market rates (currently 4–5% in many markets). For a conservative investment portfolio, 5–7% is a reasonable long-term estimate.

**Does the calculator account for inflation?**
No — it shows nominal (non-inflation-adjusted) growth. To find your real purchasing power, subtract an estimated annual inflation rate (typically 2–3%) from your expected return rate.
