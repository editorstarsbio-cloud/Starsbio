## About the Slope Calculator

The **slope** of a line measures its steepness — how much it rises or falls for every horizontal unit of movement. It is one of the most fundamental concepts in algebra, coordinate geometry, and calculus.

---

## The Slope Formula

Given two points $(x_1, y_1)$ and $(x_2, y_2)$, the slope $m$ is:

$$ m = \frac{y_2 - y_1}{x_2 - x_1} = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} = \frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}} $$

**Example:** Find the slope between $(2, 3)$ and $(6, 11)$:
$$ m = \frac{11 - 3}{6 - 2} = \frac{8}{4} = 2 $$

The line rises 2 units for every 1 unit to the right.

---

## Interpreting Slope Values

| Slope | Meaning |
|:---|:---|
| Positive ($m > 0$) | Line rises left to right |
| Negative ($m < 0$) | Line falls left to right |
| Zero ($m = 0$) | Horizontal line |
| Undefined ($m = \infty$) | Vertical line (undefined because $\Delta x = 0$) |

---

## From Slope to Line Equation

The **slope-intercept form** of a line:
$$ y = mx + b $$
where $m$ is slope and $b$ is the y-intercept (where the line crosses the y-axis).

The **point-slope form** (when you know slope $m$ and a point $(x_1, y_1)$):
$$ y - y_1 = m(x - x_1) $$

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the slope of perpendicular lines?**
Two lines are perpendicular if their slopes are negative reciprocals: $m_2 = -\frac{1}{m_1}$. A line with slope 2 is perpendicular to a line with slope $-\frac{1}{2}$.

**What is the slope of parallel lines?**
Parallel lines have exactly equal slopes. Two lines $y = 3x + 2$ and $y = 3x - 7$ are parallel because both have $m = 3$.
