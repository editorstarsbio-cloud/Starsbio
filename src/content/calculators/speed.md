## About the Speed Calculator

Speed is one of the most fundamental concepts in physics, describing how quickly an object moves from one place to another. Our **Speed Calculator** solves for speed, distance, or time when you know two of the three variables.

---

## The Formula

Speed, distance, and time are connected by one simple equation:

$$ \text{Speed} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Time}} $$

Rearranging gives you the other two variants:
$$ \text{Distance} = \text{Speed} \times \text{Time} $$
$$ \text{Time} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Speed}} $$

**Example 1 — Find Speed:** A car travels 300 km in 3 hours:
$$ \text{Speed} = \frac{300}{3} = 100 \text{ km/h} $$

**Example 2 — Find Time:** How long to drive 450 miles at 60 mph?
$$ \text{Time} = \frac{450}{60} = 7.5 \text{ hours} = 7 \text{ hr 30 min} $$

---

## Average Speed vs. Instantaneous Speed

- **Instantaneous Speed:** The speed at one specific moment (what your car's speedometer shows).
- **Average Speed:** Total distance divided by total time for an entire trip.

If you drove 100 km at 120 km/h and then 100 km at 80 km/h, your average speed is **NOT** 100 km/h:
$$ \text{Time}_1 = \frac{100}{120} = 0.833 \text{ hr}, \quad \text{Time}_2 = \frac{100}{80} = 1.25 \text{ hr} $$
$$ \text{Average Speed} = \frac{200}{0.833 + 1.25} = \frac{200}{2.083} \approx 96 \text{ km/h} $$

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the difference between speed and velocity?**
Speed is a **scalar** quantity — it only has magnitude (e.g., 60 mph). Velocity is a **vector** quantity — it has both magnitude and direction (e.g., 60 mph heading north). In everyday driving, we use speed. In physics and engineering, velocity is critical.

**How do I convert km/h to mph?**
Multiply km/h by **0.6214**. To go from mph to km/h, multiply by **1.6093**.
