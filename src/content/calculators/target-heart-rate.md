## About the Target Heart Rate Calculator

Your **Target Heart Rate (THR)** is the ideal range for your heart rate during aerobic exercise to maximize cardiovascular benefits while staying safe. Exercising within this zone is more effective than working too hard or too easy.

> **Medical Disclaimer:** This calculator provides statistical estimates for healthy adults. Consult your physician before starting a new exercise program, especially if you have cardiovascular conditions.

---

## Maximum Heart Rate Formula

The first step is estimating your **Maximum Heart Rate (MHR)** — the fastest your heart can safely beat:

$$ \text{MHR} = 220 - \text{Age} $$

*(More accurate Tanaka formula: $\text{MHR} = 208 - 0.7 \times \text{Age}$)*

**Example:** 35-year-old:
$$ \text{MHR} = 220 - 35 = 185 \text{ bpm} $$

## Target Heart Rate Zones

| Zone | Intensity | % of MHR | Heart Rate (35yr) |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| Warm Up | Very Light | 50–60% | 93–111 bpm |
| Fat Burn | Light | 60–70% | 111–130 bpm |
| Aerobic | Moderate | 70–80% | 130–148 bpm |
| Anaerobic | Hard | 80–90% | 148–167 bpm |
| Maximum | Maximum | 90–100% | 167–185 bpm |

---

## Karvonen Heart Rate Reserve Method

For a more personalized target, the Karvonen method uses your resting heart rate (RHR):

$$ \text{THR} = ((\text{MHR} - \text{RHR}) \times \%\text{Intensity}) + \text{RHR} $$

**Example:** MHR = 185, RHR = 60, target 70% intensity:
$$ \text{THR} = ((185 - 60) \times 0.70) + 60 = 87.5 + 60 = 147.5 \text{ bpm} $$

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I measure my resting heart rate?**
Measure it first thing in the morning before getting out of bed. Place two fingers on your wrist or neck and count beats for 60 seconds (or 30 seconds × 2). A typical adult RHR is 60–100 bpm. Fit athletes may have an RHR as low as 40 bpm.

**Should I always exercise at maximum heart rate?**
No. Training at 90%+ MHR is anaerobic and cannot be sustained for long. Most endurance training benefits occur in the 65–85% range. High-intensity interval training (HIIT) briefly pushes into anaerobic zones with recovery periods.
