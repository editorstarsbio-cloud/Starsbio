## About the Work Calculator (Physics)

In physics, **work** is defined as the transfer of energy that occurs when a force moves an object through a displacement. Unlike the everyday meaning of "work," the physics definition is precise: if you push a wall and it doesn't move, you did zero work — no matter how hard you pushed.

---

## The Formula

$$ W = F \times d \times \cos(\theta) $$

Where:
- **$W$** = Work (Joules, J)
- **$F$** = Force applied (Newtons, N)
- **$d$** = Displacement (meters, m)
- **$\theta$** = Angle between the force direction and the direction of motion

**When force and motion are in the same direction ($\theta = 0°$):**
$$ W = F \times d \quad (\text{simplest case, } \cos 0° = 1) $$

**Example:** Pushing a box with 50 N of force across 10 meters on a flat floor:
$$ W = 50 \times 10 = 500 \text{ J} $$

**Example with angle:** Pulling a suitcase with 60 N at a 30° angle from horizontal over 15 m:
$$ W = 60 \times 15 \times \cos(30°) = 900 \times 0.866 = 779.4 \text{ J} $$

---

## Positive, Negative, and Zero Work

- **Positive work:** Force has a component in the direction of motion (you push a box forward).
- **Negative work:** Force opposes motion (friction slowing a sliding box). 
- **Zero work:** Force is perpendicular to motion (carrying a box horizontally — gravity does zero work).

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the Work-Energy Theorem?**
The total work done on an object equals the change in its kinetic energy:
$$ W_{net} = \Delta KE = \frac{1}{2}mv_f^2 - \frac{1}{2}mv_i^2 $$

**Is work a scalar or vector?**
Work is a **scalar** — it has magnitude but no direction. Even though force and displacement are vectors, their dot product produces a scalar quantity.
