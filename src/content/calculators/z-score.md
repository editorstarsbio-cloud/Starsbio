## About the Z-Score Calculator

A **Z-score** (also called a standard score) tells you how many standard deviations a specific data point is from the mean of its distribution. It is one of the most powerful tools in statistics for comparing values from different datasets or assessing how unusual a specific result is.

---

## The Formula

$$ z = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma} $$

Where:
- **$z$** = Z-score
- **$x$** = The individual data point
- **$\mu$** = Mean of the dataset
- **$\sigma$** = Standard deviation of the dataset

**Example:** Test scores in a class have a mean of 75 and a standard deviation of 10. A student scored 92. What is their z-score?

$$ z = \frac{92 - 75}{10} = \frac{17}{10} = 1.7 $$

A z-score of **+1.7** means the student scored 1.7 standard deviations above the class average. This is approximately the top 4.5% of the class.

---

## Interpreting Z-Scores

| Z-Score | Interpretation |
|:---|:---|
| z = 0 | Exactly at the mean |
| z = +1 | One SD above the mean (84th percentile) |
| z = −1 | One SD below the mean (16th percentile) |
| z = +2 | Two SDs above the mean (97.7th percentile) |
| z = −2 | Two SDs below the mean (2.3rd percentile) |
| z > +3 or z < −3 | Extremely unusual (outlier) |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the difference between a z-score and a percentile?**
A z-score tells you how far from the mean a value is in standard deviation units. A percentile tells you what percentage of the population scored lower than that value. For a normal distribution, you convert between them using a standard normal table (or our calculator).

**What is a "good" z-score?**
Context matters. In manufacturing quality control, a z-score above 3 or below −3 typically triggers investigation. In academic testing, a z-score above +2 is exceptional.
